# Working Log

## Run: personal-crm-founders
## Date: 2026-04-14

---

### Step 1 — Signal Doc
**Status:** complete

Drew on observable founder behavior and publicly available language from founder communities (Twitter/X threads, YC forums, indie hacker discussions, personal CRM product reviews).

No primary interviews or support transcripts available. Signal is pattern-level, not quote-level. The signal doc names this limitation and keeps claims at the level of what is repeatedly observed, not verified.

Key tensions surfaced:
- Context loss is the primary pain, not contact volume
- The existing tool landscape (sales CRMs) is designed for a different job
- Trust question is about maintenance burden, not data privacy
- Audience splits meaningfully between pre-seed and post-seed founders

---

### Step 2 — Message Spine
**Status:** complete

Reduced from signal doc to three core truths.

The spine held on: this is a context problem, not a contact problem. Dropped the "network as asset" framing early — it's generic and slightly hollow. Kept the pain framing: "you know who matters, you've lost track of where you are with them."

Audience distinction preserved: pre-seed vs post-seed founders have different primary relationship types, which matters for section emphasis in the page argument but not for the core message.

---

### Step 3 — First-Page Decision
**Status:** complete

Decision: homepage.

Reasoning: founders arriving at this product already know they have a relationship management problem. They are not browsing a category — they are looking for something that fits their specific situation. The homepage job is recognition and distinction (this is not another sales CRM) rather than category education.

A "how it works" page or a features page would be premature — the recognition step has to happen first.

---

### Step 4 — Page Argument Shape
**Status:** complete

Argument order: recognition → distinction → mechanism → relationship type fit → trust answer → proof → CTA.

The shape is not standard hero-features-testimonials. It opens with the specific pain (context loss, not contact chaos) before showing the product. The distinction section earns the right to present the product by ruling out the wrong mental model (sales CRM). Proof lands after mechanism, not before.

Named four sections as unnecessary: integration list in the hero, pricing in the first pass, a "for teams" section, a generic testimonial carousel.

---

### Step 5 — Proof Map
**Status:** complete

Main proof gaps: no named founder testimonials, no quantitative relationship data, no VC or advisor endorsements available for this draft. 

Draft constrained accordingly: testimonials templated but not filled with invented names, quantitative claims avoided, mechanism described behaviorally rather than by feature specification.

---

### Step 6 — Homepage Draft
**Status:** complete

Draft follows the approved argument shape. Proof gaps honored — no invented specifics. Two soft weak spots noted inline in the draft where proof would strengthen the copy if it became available.

---

### Overall judgment
The run worked. The process surfaced the right wedge (context vs. contacts) quickly and kept the draft from defaulting into sales CRM language or generic productivity copy.

Biggest remaining gap: the proof map is thin. If real founder testimonials or usage data became available, the draft's trust section could get materially stronger. The current draft compensates with specificity of description rather than social proof.

Upstream recommendation if this draft is used: run at least 3-5 founder interviews before publishing. The signal doc assumptions should be tested against real founder language before the copy locks.
