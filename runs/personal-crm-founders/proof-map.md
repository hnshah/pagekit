# Proof Map — Personal CRM for Startup Founders Homepage

## Page
- name: Homepage — first-pass draft
- audience: Startup founders (primarily pre-seed and early post-seed)
- job: Produce recognition of the context-loss problem, make the sales CRM distinction, show the mechanism, and earn enough trust to convert to sign-up

---

## Required proof by argument section

### Section 1 — Recognition (hero)
**Proof needed:** None in the traditional sense. This section earns credibility through accuracy of problem description, not social proof.
**What this means for drafting:** The lead line has to be specific enough that a founder with the problem immediately recognizes it. If it sounds like a general productivity pitch, this section fails before proof can help.
**Current status:** Drafting can proceed. No external proof required.

---

### Section 2 — The wrong tool problem
**Proof needed:** Implicit — the reader's own experience of trying a sales CRM and finding it wrong.
**What this means for drafting:** The argument is credible when it names the specific mismatch (deal stages for investor relationships) in a way that feels like the reader's own observation, not a competitor attack.
**Current status:** Drafting can proceed. No external proof required, but the claim must be specific and accurate.

---

### Section 3 — Mechanism (how it works)
**Proof needed:** Product accuracy — the behaviors described must match what the product actually does.
**What this means for drafting:** The mechanism can only be described at the level of what is verifiably true. If email sync is real, name it. If it requires manual logging, say so. Do not claim zero-effort if there is effort.
**Current gap:** The actual product mechanism is not specified in the source materials for this run. The draft will describe plausible behaviors (quick logging, relationship age visibility, context surfacing before a call) but must be verified against the real product before publishing.
**Risk:** If the mechanism description overpromises, the trust section (Section 5) cannot recover it. This is the highest proof risk in the draft.

---

### Section 4 — Relationship type fit
**Proof needed:** Product empathy — the relationship categories described must match what the product actually handles.
**What this means for drafting:** The investor / advisor / customer / potential hire categorization needs to reflect real product categories, not assumed ones. If the product doesn't have founder-specific categories built in, the copy should not imply it does.
**Current gap:** Relationship type categories are unverified. Draft will name the relationship types descriptively; verification needed before publication.
**Current status:** Drafting can proceed with clear verification flag.

---

### Section 5 — The maintenance question (trust)
**Proof needed:** An honest and accurate answer to the data entry burden question.
**What this means for drafting:** This is the section where the draft is most exposed. A vague answer ("it's easy to maintain") will fail. A specific answer (email sync, 30-second log, weekly nudges) requires knowing what the product actually does.
**Current gap:** Maintenance mechanism is not specified. The draft will give an honest framing ("this works if you invest X; it won't if you don't") rather than a specific feature claim.
**Risk:** If the maintenance answer is too honest without a compelling counter, this section could slow conversions. If it's not honest enough, founders will bounce after signup when they discover the effort required.

---

### Section 6 — Proof (founder use cases)
**Proof needed:** Two or three specific founder use cases with scenario-level detail — not generic satisfaction testimonials.

**Use-case proof needed:**
- An investor relationship example: tracking conversation state through a fundraise, knowing which investors are warm vs waiting, not letting follow-up slip
- An advisor relationship example: maintaining regular check-ins, having context before a call, catching a relationship before it went cold
- A customer relationship example: tracking a key customer relationship outside of a support ticket system

**Testimonial proof needed:** Named founders with specific outcomes, not ratings.

**Current gap:** No real founder testimonials available for this run. Proof section will be drafted with placeholder structure showing the format the testimonial should take. The copy will name the type of evidence needed rather than invent specifics.

**What cannot be invented:** Founder names, company names, specific outcomes, timeframes. Any of these invented will undermine trust if discovered and are not appropriate for publication.

---

### Section 7 — CTA
**Proof needed:** None beyond what prior sections have earned.
**Risk:** If sections 1-6 haven't done their work, a CTA is just a button. This section cannot compensate for upstream weakness.

---

## Weak spots — where the draft could drift into generic or unsupported language

**Weak spot 1: The mechanism description (Section 3)**
High risk of vague description that sounds like any CRM. The draft needs to name specific behaviors, not abstract capabilities. "Keeps your relationships organized" is the failure mode. "Shows you that you haven't spoken to [investor] in six weeks and your last conversation ended with you promising to send a deck" is the target.

**Weak spot 2: The maintenance answer (Section 5)**
High risk of either overclaiming (sounds effortless, isn't) or underclaiming (sounds like work, discourages sign-up). The draft needs to know what the actual maintenance model is before this section can be written honestly. Current draft will flag this explicitly.

**Weak spot 3: The relationship type section (Section 4)**
Medium risk of sounding like a feature list rather than a recognition moment. The failure mode is bullet points with feature names. The target is a paragraph that makes a founder feel understood without listing capabilities.

**Weak spot 4: Proof placeholders (Section 6)**
If the placeholder structure is too close to copy, a reviewer might approve it without filling it in. The draft will mark placeholders explicitly to prevent this.
