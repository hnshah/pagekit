## Raw output (as produced)

### 1. What the product appears to be

A relationship-state tracker for startup founders. Not a CRM in the Salesforce sense — no pipeline, no deals, no lead scoring. The core unit is a "relationship state card" that captures where a founder currently stands with a specific person: when they last talked, what was said, what follow-up is owed, and when to reach back out. The product is designed for the 50–100 relationships that carry the most weight in a founder's company — investors, advisors, early customers, potential hires, key press — not for the thousands of contacts in a sales database.

The product's stated origin is a founder building something they needed personally, which is a useful signal: the wedge came from lived experience, not market analysis.

### 2. Likely target audiences

**Primary:** Early-stage startup founders, seed to Series A. These founders carry high-stakes relationship portfolios that they manage largely alone or with one co-founder. The relationships matter enormously (an investor who goes cold on you is a fundraising problem; an advisor you've stopped updating is a missed leverage point), but the volume is manageable — small enough to track, large enough that memory and email threads fail.

**Secondary:** Solo operators, indie founders, and one-person businesses who have a professional network they need to maintain but no sales-process reason to use a sales CRM.

**Out of scope from the brief:** Sales teams, partnerships managers, enterprise buyers. The product's explicit "not this" list rules these out.

### 3. Recurring pains or friction

- **Going dark on relationships that matter**: A founder realizes they haven't spoken to an important investor in three months. They intended to send an update. It never happened.
- **Not knowing where you left things**: Before reaching out to an advisor, a founder has to hunt through email threads to remember what was discussed and what was promised. The context is there, just scattered.
- **Follow-up debt accumulating**: Founders carry a mental list of "I should reach back out to X." That list grows and ages. Some items never get acted on because there's no external prompt.
- **Sales CRMs feel like the wrong tool**: Salesforce and HubSpot are built for deal pipelines. A founder trying to use them for relationship management is shoehorning a different job into a tool designed for a different user.
- **Spreadsheets degrade over time**: Founder-built contact trackers are accurate for a month, then slowly abandoned because updating them requires deliberate effort that disappears when things get busy.

### 4. Desired outcomes

- Know immediately, for any important relationship, where things stand.
- Have no relationship "go dark" without a prompt — catch the drift before it becomes a gap.
- Be able to reach out to someone with context already loaded, not reconstructed from inbox search.
- Remove the mental overhead of maintaining a relationship-state map internally.
- If co-founder: share relational context so both people have the same picture with key investors and advisors.

### 5. Trust questions or buying resistance

- **Data privacy**: "You're importing my Gmail contact history. What happens to that data?" SOC 2 is in progress, not certified yet. This matters to security-conscious founders.
- **Fragility / lock-in**: "What if you shut down? I've loaded my relationships into this." Data export always available — the honest answer to this question.
- **Does it add another inbox?**: "I already have too many tools. Will this actually help or will I just have one more thing to update?"
- **Maintenance doubt**: Some founders will recognize the problem and still doubt whether they'll maintain any system consistently.
- **Pricing**: $19/month is low but not free. The free tier (25 relationships) may be the right entry for skeptics.

### 6. Native phrases worth keeping

- "gone dark" — the failure mode of a relationship that mattered going quiet
- "where you are with someone" — the specific state the product tracks
- "follow-up rot" / "follow-up debt" — the accumulating pile of owed outreach
- "the 50 relationships that matter" — natural framing for the product's scope
- "context fade" — what happens between a meeting and the next conversation when details blur
- "relationship health" — used in the weekly digest; specific enough to keep
- "went cold" — another founder-native phrase for a relationship that has drifted

### 7. Generic language to avoid

- "Stay connected" — too vague
- "Manage your network" — LinkedIn register, wrong for this product
- "Build stronger relationships" — could be on any CRM
- "Never miss a follow-up again" — deadline-management framing; understates the problem
- "Relationship intelligence" — enterprise-sales register
- "The CRM for founders" — category-default; doesn't name the specific gap

### 8. What still feels unknown

- How founders actually discover this product: founder forums, investor referral, Twitter/X, search?
- Whether "200 on the waitlist" reads as validation or small.
- How long it takes a new user to find the product useful versus experiencing maintaining-another-thing fatigue.
- Whether the co-founder team plan has a meaningfully different first message from the solo plan.
- What the most common reason is that founders who would use this don't start.

### 9. Weak claims to avoid early

- "Me CRM will transform how you manage relationships" — transformation claim, no evidence
- "Built for founders, by founders" — category-default phrase everyone uses
- "The only CRM designed for founders" — exclusivity claim not verified
- "Keep every relationship warm" — vague
- Any specific outcome claims (increase funding probability, close deals faster) — no data
- "Founder-first design" — means nothing specific
