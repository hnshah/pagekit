# First-Page Decision — Personal CRM for Startup Founders

## Candidate first page
Homepage.

## Why this page first
The homepage earns its place because the recognition job has to happen before anything else is useful. Founders arriving at this product already know they have a problem with relationship tracking — they are not browsing a category. What they don't know yet is whether this product understands their specific problem or is just another sales CRM with different branding.

The homepage's job is to produce recognition ("this is the right framing of my problem") and distinction ("this is not a sales CRM"). Those two things have to land before a features page, a pricing page, or a how-it-works page can do any useful work.

## Page job
Help the founder understand:
1. This product is built around context loss, not contact volume — and that's the right problem
2. This is not a sales CRM; it is designed for the relationship types founders actually manage
3. There is a credible mechanism by which this solves the problem
4. Other founders with similar relationship patterns have found it useful

And help them do: sign up or request early access.

## Why this page before other pages
- A features page assumes the visitor already believes the product understands their problem. That belief hasn't been earned yet.
- A how-it-works page assumes the visitor is past the "is this for me?" question. They aren't.
- A pricing page is premature without recognition.

The homepage must do the recognition and distinction work. Everything else follows once that's done.

## Visitor state on arrival
The founder arrives having either:
- Seen this mentioned by another founder and is checking if it's worth their time
- Searched for something like "personal CRM for founders" or "relationship tracker startup"
- Just had a moment where a relationship fell through the cracks and is actively looking for a solution

In all three cases, they arrive with a problem in mind, not with a solution in mind. The page should meet them there, not present the product first.

## Main trust burden
"Is this worth setting up and maintaining?" — The biggest trust question is not about data privacy or pricing. It is about maintenance. Founders have tried and abandoned CRMs before. The page needs to answer the maintenance objection honestly, not dismiss it.

## Main proof burden
Specific founder use cases that show what "knowing where you are with someone" actually looks like in practice — an investor conversation tracked through to a follow-up, an advisor relationship that didn't go cold because of a timely reminder. Generic testimonials will not do this work. Specificity of scenario is what earns the proof.

## What this decision rules out for now
- A "for teams" page or team CRM angle — this is personal use only in the first pass
- An integrations or technical capabilities page — mechanism must be shown behaviorally, not by feature list
- A comparison page (vs HubSpot, vs Salesforce) — the homepage will make the sales CRM distinction once, clearly, without turning into a feature comparison
- A use-cases page — the homepage covers the primary use cases rather than routing to a separate page
