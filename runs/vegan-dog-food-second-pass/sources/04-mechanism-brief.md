# Mechanism Brief — Second Pass

## Why this brief exists
The nutritional-credibility question is the core trust question.
The mechanism by which Kind Bowl answers it cannot be hand-waved.

## The mechanism in plain language

### 1. Formulation target
Kind Bowl formulates to the AAFCO Dog Food Nutrient Profile for adult maintenance.
AAFCO profiles are the nutrient benchmarks used by most commercial dog foods in the US to make a "complete and balanced" statement.

### 2. Plant-based formulation specifics
Because plant proteins by themselves do not naturally hit AAFCO amino acid minimums for dogs, Kind Bowl:
- blends pea protein, brown rice, flaxseed and other plant ingredients
- adds synthetic methionine and lysine to complete the amino acid profile
- supplements taurine and L-carnitine
- adds algae-derived DHA (an omega-3 source that does not require fish)
- supplements vitamin B12 and vitamin D3 using a vegan-compatible D3 source

### 3. Verification
- third-party lab tests the finished product's amino acid profile against AAFCO minimums
- every batch is tested for nutrient levels before shipment

### 4. Who designed it
- a board-certified veterinary nutritionist (ACVN diplomate) consulted on formulation
- this is a working relationship, not a one-time review

## What this mechanism does not do
- it does not prove long-term health outcomes
- it does not replace an AAFCO feeding trial
- it does not demonstrate that plant-based is better than animal-based
- it does not generalize to puppies, pregnant dogs or dogs with medical conditions

## Language the page can earn
- "formulated with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist"
- "formulated to meet AAFCO adult-maintenance nutrient profiles"
- "amino acid profile verified by third-party testing"
- "for adult dogs"

## Language the page cannot earn
- "vet approved" (without specifying the consulting relationship)
- "clinically proven"
- "healthiest food for your dog"
- "suitable for all life stages"
