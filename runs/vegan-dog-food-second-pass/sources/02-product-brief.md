# Product Brief — Second Pass

## Product
Kind Bowl — a plant-based dog food subscription. Fictional brand used as a training object for PageKit.

## One-line description
Kind Bowl makes plant-based dog food formulated by a board-certified veterinary nutritionist, delivered on a subscription cadence, for adult dogs whose owners want a plant-based diet that still meets AAFCO adult-maintenance standards.

## Product facts (ground truth for this run)

### Format
- dry kibble, shipped monthly
- one adult-maintenance recipe at launch
- no puppy, senior or therapeutic formulas at launch

### Ingredients (representative)
- pea protein
- brown rice
- sweet potato
- flaxseed
- algae-derived DHA
- taurine and L-carnitine supplementation
- added synthetic methionine and lysine to complete the amino acid profile
- B12 and vitamin D3 (D3 in a vegan-compatible form)

### Formulation
- recipe developed with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist (ACVN diplomate)
- formulated to meet AAFCO Dog Food Nutrient Profile for adult maintenance
- amino acid profile tested by a third-party lab against AAFCO minimums

### Manufacturing
- co-packed at a facility that makes multiple specialty pet-food brands
- each batch is tested for nutrient levels before shipment

### What Kind Bowl has done
- AAFCO adult-maintenance nutrient profile compliance (by formulation and third-party amino acid testing)
- consulted with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist on formulation

### What Kind Bowl has not done
- no AAFCO feeding trial (feeding trials are a separate, more expensive validation path)
- no peer-reviewed long-term health outcome study
- no broad veterinary endorsement
- no claims about treating or preventing any health condition
- no claims about outcomes for individual dogs

### Pricing
- roughly $3 to $6 per day for most adult dogs, depending on dog weight
- subscription cadence: 4 weeks by default, adjustable

### Audience they currently serve
- adult dogs, not puppies and not dogs with specific medical conditions
- owners who are already values-aligned with plant-based eating

## Constraints on copy
- do not invent veterinary proof
- do not claim "vet approved" unless backed by the actual consulting relationship language
- do not claim health outcomes for any dog
- do not claim superiority over conventional dog food
- do not imply the product is suitable for puppies, pregnant dogs or dogs with medical conditions
- do not imply every dog thrives on this food; the honest framing is "meets adult-maintenance nutritional standards"

## Category tension
The real tension is not convenience or ethics.
It is: "Can I trust a plant-based dog food to actually feed my dog properly, when the category has more marketing than evidence?"

Kind Bowl's honest answer is:
- meets the same AAFCO nutritional adequacy standard that conventional adult dog foods meet
- formulated by a board-certified veterinary nutritionist
- third-party amino acid testing

That is a real, specific, defensible trust claim.
It is also narrower than "healthiest food for your dog."
