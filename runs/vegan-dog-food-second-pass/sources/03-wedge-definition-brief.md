# Wedge Definition Brief — Second Pass

## Why this exists
The first-pass wedge framed the buyer as values-aligned and the pain as ethical tension.
That framing is not wrong, but it leaves the hardest trust question unsaid.
Second pass re-centers the wedge on the thing Kind Bowl actually has to earn: nutritional credibility.

## What Kind Bowl should be treated as for this run
A plant-based dog food that is taking the nutritional-adequacy question seriously enough to formulate against AAFCO standards with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist, for adult dogs only.

## First audience hypothesis
Primary:
- dog owners who are already vegan or vegetarian themselves
- specifically, the subset who have looked into plant-based dog food once and bounced off the trust question

Secondary (explicitly not served yet):
- owners of puppies, pregnant dogs or dogs on therapeutic diets
- owners who are curious but not values-aligned

## Sharp pain framing
The real pain is not "I want plant-based dog food."
The real pain is: "I want this choice to be nutritionally defensible, not just values-aligned, and the category is full of soft marketing that does not help me decide."

## What this is closer to
- a nutritionally-audited adult dog food that happens to be plant-based

## What this is farther from
- a lifestyle brand
- a health-outcomes brand
- an anti-conventional-dog-food brand

## First-page implication
The first page should optimize for:
- nutritional credibility
- specific, checkable claims
- adult-only scope made visible
- honest limits on what Kind Bowl has and has not done

Do not assume this page is a homepage.

## Claims we still cannot make
- "healthier than conventional food"
- "vet-approved" in a broad sense
- "complete nutrition for any dog"
- any outcome claim about individual dogs
- any therapeutic claim
