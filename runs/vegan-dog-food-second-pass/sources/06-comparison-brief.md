# Comparison Brief — Second Pass

## What Kind Bowl is implicitly compared against

### Conventional kibble
- most widely used
- meets AAFCO nutrient profiles
- backed by decades of feeding data
- not aligned with plant-based values
- Kind Bowl's honest positioning: Kind Bowl meets the same AAFCO nutrient-profile standard that most conventional adult kibbles meet

### Other plant-based dog food brands
- several exist
- positioning varies: some lean "lifestyle," some lean "health-outcomes," a few lean "veterinary-nutritionist formulated"
- Kind Bowl's honest positioning: Kind Bowl is in the nutritionist-formulated and third-party-tested subset, not the lifestyle subset
- not all plant-based brands disclose AAFCO profile compliance or show third-party testing; Kind Bowl does

### Therapeutic veterinary diets
- clinically indicated foods prescribed by vets
- not what Kind Bowl is
- the page must make this distinction visible; Kind Bowl is not a therapeutic or prescription product

### Raw, fresh or home-cooked
- separate category, different trust questions
- not Kind Bowl's primary comparison

## What Kind Bowl should not do
- frame itself as superior to conventional dog food on health outcomes
- frame itself as superior to other plant-based brands without specific, checkable differences
- frame itself as a therapeutic product
- compete on aesthetics instead of nutritional credibility

## What the first page can fairly claim as comparative framing
- "meets the same AAFCO adult-maintenance nutrient profile standard used by most adult dog foods"
- "formulated by a board-certified veterinary nutritionist"
- "third-party amino acid testing"
- "for adult dogs, not puppies or therapeutic diets"

These are specific, checkable and narrower than category-average plant-based dog food marketing.
