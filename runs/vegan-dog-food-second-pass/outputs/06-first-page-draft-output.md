# How we built a plant-based dog food to an AAFCO adult-maintenance standard

### Scope

Kind Bowl is a plant-based dry kibble for adult dogs, formulated to meet the AAFCO Dog Food Nutrient Profile for adult maintenance.

This page exists to show the work behind that claim.

It is not a homepage, and it is not a sales page. If you want to see the product details or pricing, links are at the end.

### What we formulated to

Kind Bowl is formulated to meet the AAFCO Dog Food Nutrient Profile for adult maintenance.

AAFCO's profile is the nutrient benchmark that most adult dog foods in the US use when they make a "complete and balanced" statement. There are two ways a food can show that it meets the profile: by formulation (with laboratory testing) and by a six-month AAFCO feeding trial.

Kind Bowl's compliance is by formulation and third-party amino acid testing. It is not compliance by feeding trial. A feeding trial is a separate, longer validation path we have not completed.

We think that distinction matters, so we say it on the first page.

### Who formulated it

The Kind Bowl recipe was formulated in an ongoing consulting relationship with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist — specifically, a diplomate of the American College of Veterinary Nutrition (ACVN).

ACVN diplomate status is a specialty certification for veterinarians who have completed a residency in clinical nutrition and passed the ACVN board exam. It is the credential most commonly cited when a brand wants to say their food was formulated by a specialist, rather than by a generalist vet.

We will not say "vet approved." Vet approval is not a real regulatory status, and the phrase gets used too loosely in this category to mean anything.

### How a plant-based formulation meets adult-maintenance requirements

Plant ingredients alone do not naturally meet AAFCO amino acid minimums for dogs.

To complete the profile, Kind Bowl adds:

- **methionine and lysine**, two amino acids that plant proteins are typically low in for dogs
- **taurine and L-carnitine**, both relevant to heart muscle health in dogs and both commonly supplemented in plant-based formulations
- **DHA from algae**, an omega-3 source that does not require fish
- **vitamin D3 from a vegan-compatible source**, because D2 is not well-utilized by dogs

The rest of the recipe is built from pea protein, brown rice, sweet potato, flaxseed and standard vitamin and mineral premixes.

This is what it takes to bring a plant-based formulation up to AAFCO adult-maintenance requirements. It is not a health claim; it is a nutritional-adequacy claim.

### What we test

Two things, both documented:

- the amino acid profile of the finished product is tested against AAFCO adult-maintenance minimums by a third-party laboratory
- every batch is tested for nutrient levels before it ships

We are not claiming this replaces a feeding trial. It does not. It is the verification layer that sits underneath formulation.

### What we have not done

This section is here because the category does not usually put it on a first page.

Kind Bowl has not:

- completed an AAFCO feeding trial
- published long-term health outcome studies in dogs fed Kind Bowl
- been formulated for puppies, pregnant or nursing dogs, or senior dogs with medical conditions
- been formulated as a therapeutic or prescription diet
- gathered a meaningful body of customer feedback yet

If any of these is what you need, Kind Bowl may not be the right choice today.
