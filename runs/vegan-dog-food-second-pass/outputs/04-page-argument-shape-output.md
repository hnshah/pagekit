# Raw Output 04 — Page Argument Shape

## Model
Claude (Claude Code, Opus 4.6)

## Raw output (as produced)

## 1. What the visitor needs to understand first
Three things, in the first screen:
- this is plant-based dog food for adult dogs only
- it is formulated to the AAFCO adult-maintenance nutrient profile with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist
- this is a nutritional-adequacy page, not a marketing page — the goal is to show the work, not to sell

This answers the "am I in the right place" question and the "is this brand going to actually explain itself" question at the same time.

## 2. What must be earned next
After the orienting statement, earn specificity:
- which AAFCO profile and which flavor of compliance (formulation + third-party testing, not feeding trial)
- the name and credential of the consulting nutritionist (ACVN diplomate)
- how a plant-based formulation is brought up to AAFCO amino acid minimums (the specific supplementation strategy)
- what has and has not been tested

Each of these is a small trust increment. None of them asks for a subscription.

## 3. Argument order
1. Scope and positioning in one honest sentence
2. What we formulated to — the specific AAFCO standard
3. Who formulated it — the consulting nutritionist credential, specifically
4. How a plant-based formulation meets adult-maintenance requirements — the mechanism
5. What we test and how often
6. What we have not done and are not claiming
7. Where to go next if this is enough for now

## 4. Sections the page needs
- **Scope statement** (very short, near the top)
- **AAFCO compliance, made specific** (what profile, what compliance flavor)
- **Who formulated it** (nutritionist credential, not "our vets")
- **The mechanism** (how plant-based meets amino-acid and micronutrient requirements)
- **Testing** (third-party amino acid testing and per-batch testing)
- **What we have not done** (honest limits, explicit)
- **A small, unhyped next step** (e.g., "see the ingredient list" or "see the full formulation notes")

## 5. Sections that would be unnecessary or harmful here
- a hero with a brand promise
- a founder story
- lifestyle imagery-driven section
- ethics or sustainability section (belongs elsewhere, not on the first page)
- customer testimonials (the product does not have a meaningful customer review corpus yet; showing thin testimonials would undercut the credibility argument)
- a subscription-value section
- a pricing section
- a "benefits" section in the category-default sense

## 6. Where trust should land
Trust should land on the credentialing and compliance sections (who formulated it, what standard, what testing). Trust does not land on brand voice or aesthetics for this audience.

## 7. Where proof should land
Proof should land on:
- AAFCO profile compliance — named specifically
- consulting nutritionist credential — named specifically (ACVN diplomate)
- third-party amino acid testing — named specifically
- per-batch testing — named specifically
- the "what we have not done" section — which is itself a proof-honesty move

## 8. Drafting constraints
- no bare "vet approved"
- no "clinically proven"
- no health-superiority comparisons
- no life-stage breadth language
- no outcome claims
- do not lead with ethics
- do not use "guilt-free" or "feel good" framing
- do not bury the adult-only scope
- do not hide the not-yet-done list
- keep section headings boring; the content carries the weight, not the headings
- the page can be longer than a homepage; it cannot be wider

## Judgment
Strong shape.
This is visibly not a homepage shape.
The main risk remains that the page reads technical. The mitigation is to make the scope statement and the named credential the first things the visitor sees, so the technical sections are earned, not endured.
