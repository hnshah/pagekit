# Raw Output 05 — Proof Map

## Model
Claude (Claude Code, Opus 4.6)

## Raw output (as produced)

## 1. What the page can honestly claim now
- "formulated to meet the AAFCO Dog Food Nutrient Profile for adult maintenance"
- "formulated with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist (ACVN diplomate)"
- "amino acid profile verified against AAFCO minimums by a third-party lab"
- "every batch is tested for nutrient levels before shipment"
- "for adult dogs" (and derivative phrasing)
- "this compliance is by formulation and testing, not by AAFCO feeding trial"
- specific supplementation language grounded in the mechanism brief (methionine, lysine, taurine, L-carnitine, algae DHA, vegan D3)

## 2. What it does not yet have proof for
- long-term health outcomes in dogs fed Kind Bowl
- performance vs. conventional food on any health measure
- suitability for puppies, senior dogs with certain conditions, pregnant dogs, nursing dogs
- suitability for therapeutic needs
- broad veterinary endorsement
- customer satisfaction or palatability (no meaningful corpus)
- any "our dogs thrive" claim

## 3. Safe credibility moves
- name the AAFCO standard in full
- name the compliance flavor explicitly (formulation + third-party testing; no feeding trial)
- name the nutritionist's specific credential (ACVN diplomate) and their role (consulting on formulation, ongoing)
- name the third-party testing practice
- name the per-batch testing practice
- explicitly section off the "what we have not done" list
- explicitly section off the "not for" list (puppies, therapeutic, etc.)

## 4. Risky or inflated moves to avoid
- "vet approved" without specifying who and in what role
- "clinically tested" or "clinically proven"
- any "healthiest" framing
- any comparison to conventional kibble on health outcomes
- any implication that formulation compliance is equivalent to a feeding trial
- "all life stages"
- any "dogs thrive" or "dogs love it" claim without the data to support it
- mentioning any individual dog anecdote as proof
- any "backed by science" or "research shows" language for research Kind Bowl did not conduct

## 5. Where proof lands in the approved argument shape (section by section)

### Scope statement
No heavy proof here; its job is accurate positioning.
Safe language: "plant-based dog food for adult dogs, formulated to meet AAFCO adult-maintenance nutrient requirements."

### AAFCO compliance, made specific
- name the exact profile
- name the exact compliance flavor
- name what feeding trial compliance would mean and note Kind Bowl does not claim it

### Who formulated it
- name the credential (ACVN diplomate) in full
- describe the working relationship (consulting on formulation, ongoing)
- do not claim broader "team of vets" without basis

### The mechanism
- specific supplementation (methionine, lysine, taurine, L-carnitine)
- algae DHA as an omega-3 source
- vegan-compatible D3 source
- the reason each supplement is present in AAFCO-profile terms

### Testing
- name the third-party amino acid test
- name what it tests against (AAFCO minimums)
- name the per-batch testing practice

### What we have not done
This section is itself a proof move. The page becomes more credible by naming:
- no AAFCO feeding trial
- no long-term outcome study
- no breed-specific or age-range-specific guidance beyond "adult"
- no therapeutic claims

### Small, unhyped next step
No proof weight here. The step is a link to a deeper resource (for example ingredient detail or the consulting nutritionist bio page), not a subscription CTA.

## 6. What each approved section can say safely

### Scope statement
- "plant-based dog food for adult dogs, formulated to meet AAFCO adult-maintenance nutrient requirements."
- "this page shows the work behind that claim."
- what it should not say: anything about lifestyle, values or sustainability.

### AAFCO compliance section
- "Kind Bowl is formulated to meet the AAFCO Dog Food Nutrient Profile for adult maintenance."
- "this is compliance by formulation and third-party amino acid testing. It is not compliance by AAFCO feeding trial."
- what it should not say: "AAFCO approved" or "AAFCO certified" (neither is how AAFCO works).

### Who formulated it
- "formulated with [Name, ACVN diplomate]" (where the name exists in the real product; for this fictional run, leave the name as a placeholder and name the credential)
- "the consulting relationship is ongoing and covers formulation."
- what it should not say: "our vets approve" or "team of veterinary experts."

### Mechanism
- "plant ingredients alone do not naturally meet AAFCO amino acid minimums for dogs. Kind Bowl adds methionine, lysine, taurine and L-carnitine to complete the profile."
- "DHA comes from algae. Vitamin D3 comes from a vegan-compatible source."
- what it should not say: anything suggesting superiority or uniqueness; the mechanism is specific to vegan formulation, not universally better.

### Testing
- "the amino acid profile of the finished product is tested against AAFCO minimums by a third-party lab."
- "every batch is tested for nutrient levels before shipment."
- what it should not say: "rigorously tested" without naming what is tested or how often.

### What we have not done
- "we have not completed an AAFCO feeding trial. Feeding trials are a separate, longer validation path."
- "we have not published long-term outcome studies."
- "Kind Bowl is not a therapeutic product."
- "Kind Bowl is not formulated for puppies, pregnant or nursing dogs, or dogs on medically restricted diets."
- what it should not say: soften these limits with "yet" unless there is a real plan; do not imply the limits will go away.

### Unhyped next step
- a link to a supporting page that continues the credibility story (e.g., ingredient detail or formulation notes)
- what it should not say: a subscription offer, a discount, a "join now" button. The page is not for that.

## Judgment
The proof map is narrow on purpose. If the draft tries to widen the page beyond this, it will overclaim.
