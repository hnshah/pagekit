# Raw Output 03 — First-Page Decision

## Model
Claude (Claude Code, Opus 4.6)

## Raw output (as produced)

## 1. Best first page candidate
A **nutritional-adequacy page** — a page whose entire job is to show how Kind Bowl answers the "is this actually defensible for my adult dog" question, in specific checkable terms, and to name the scope.

Working title for this page: "How we built a plant-based dog food to an AAFCO adult-maintenance standard."

## 2. Why this page first
The spine names one trust question, and it is not a homepage-shaped question. It is a nutritional-credibility question.
A homepage that starts with brand, hero imagery and three category benefits will earn the category's default skepticism, not answer it.
A page that leads with the mechanism and the scope can answer the spine's actual question on the first visit.
The category's marketing is soft on exactly this, so a first page that leads with the mechanism is also the clearest differentiator from the rest of plant-based dog food.

## 3. The page's job
The page should, in one read:
- commit to the AAFCO flavor of compliance (formulation + third-party amino acid testing, not a feeding trial)
- show the mechanism that makes plant-based formulation meet adult-maintenance requirements
- name the consulting veterinary nutritionist credential specifically
- name the scope (adult dogs only, not puppies, not therapeutic)
- name what Kind Bowl has not done
- leave the reader able to decide whether they want to continue

The page's job is not to sell a subscription on this visit. Subscription belongs on a later page once trust has landed.

## 4. Visitor state on arrival
- already vegan or vegetarian themselves
- has looked into plant-based dog food before
- has probably bounced off at least one plant-based brand whose claims felt soft
- arrives primed to detect marketing language; skepticism is the default
- wants to be told specifically what has been done, not promised outcomes

## 5. Main trust burden
Convince a skeptical values-aligned adult-dog owner that this plant-based food has taken nutritional adequacy seriously in a way they can verify — and do it without using the category's soft trust language.

## 6. Main proof burden
- specific AAFCO profile met (adult maintenance)
- specific compliance path used (formulation + third-party amino acid testing, not feeding trial)
- specific credential of the consulting nutritionist (ACVN diplomate)
- specific supplementation strategy (methionine, lysine, taurine, L-carnitine, algae DHA, vegan D3)
- specific per-batch testing practice
- specific scope limits (adult only, not therapeutic)
- explicit naming of what has not been done

## 7. What this decision rules out for now
- a homepage-first approach
- any page that opens on ethics, sustainability or lifestyle framing
- any page that opens on subscription mechanics
- any page that requires breadth across puppies, seniors and therapeutic diets
- any page that needs a broader veterinary endorsement than the single consulting nutritionist

## 8. Candidates considered and rejected

### Candidate A — homepage
Rejected.
A generic homepage rhythm (hero, benefits, social proof, CTA) does not answer the actual trust question. It invites the category's default skepticism because the reader arrives already expecting soft marketing.
A homepage can exist later, once the nutritional credibility has landed somewhere specific enough to link back to it.

### Candidate B — "Why plant-based dog food" category-explainer page
Rejected.
This kind of page argues for the category, not for Kind Bowl. Category-level arguing lands as advocacy and makes Kind Bowl look like a lifestyle brand. It also would pull the page toward ethics framing, which the spine explicitly deprioritizes.

### Candidate C — ingredient / formula detail page
Considered but weaker than the chosen page.
An ingredient page can show the what but not the how or the credential. It does not make the AAFCO compliance visible, and it lets "formulated with a veterinary nutritionist" read as a badge rather than a method. Ingredient detail belongs inside the nutritional-adequacy page, not as the first page.

### Candidate D — founder / brand story page
Rejected.
Founder stories cannot carry nutritional credibility. The audience on arrival is more skeptical of story than of mechanism.

## Judgment
Strong decision.
The page name is narrower than "homepage" and the job is narrower than a homepage's job, which is what this object needs.
The real risk is that this page sounds like a long-form technical explainer and loses a reader who wanted a simpler entry point. The argument shape step should protect against that by putting the scope and the specific credentials near the top, not at the bottom.
