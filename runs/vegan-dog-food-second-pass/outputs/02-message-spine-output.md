# Raw Output 02 — Message Spine

## Model
Claude (Claude Code, Opus 4.6)

## Raw output (as produced)

## 1. Core truth
Kind Bowl is a plant-based adult-maintenance dog food formulated to AAFCO nutrient profiles with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist and verified by third-party amino acid testing. It is not a lifestyle product, not a therapeutic product, and not for puppies or medically restricted dogs at launch.

## 2. Target audience
Adult-dog owners who are already vegan or vegetarian themselves and have looked into plant-based dog food at least once, specifically the subset who bounced off the soft-evidence problem in the category.

## 3. Sharp pain statement
The hard part is not finding plant-based dog food.
The hard part is being able to tell which plant-based dog food has taken nutritional adequacy seriously, because the category's marketing hides which brands have done the work and which have not.

## 4. Desired shift or outcome
Move the reader from:
- "plant-based dog food is risky and the category is noisy"
to:
- "this specific brand has taken the nutritional adequacy question seriously in a way I can check, and I know what it is and is not for."

## 5. Strongest language to preserve
- formulated by a board-certified veterinary nutritionist
- AAFCO adult-maintenance nutrient profile
- third-party amino acid testing
- for adult dogs
- what we have done and what we have not done
- nutritional adequacy (not "nutritional superiority")

## 6. Page-level message implications
- any first page should lead with nutritional credibility, not ethics
- any first page should commit to the specific AAFCO flavor of compliance (formulation + testing, not feeding trial) and not hide it
- any first page should name what Kind Bowl has not done
- any first page should make the adult-only scope visible
- any first page should name the consulting nutritionist credential, not gesture at "vets"
- no page should use bare "vet approved"
- no page should imply outcomes for individual dogs
- no page should frame Kind Bowl as superior to conventional food on health outcomes

## 7. Key trust question to answer
"How do I know this is actually nutritionally defensible for my adult dog, in a way I can verify rather than take on faith?"

## 8. Weak directions to avoid
- leading with ethics or sustainability
- "guilt-free" or "feel good" framing
- bare "vet approved"
- implied health superiority over conventional food
- subscription-first framing before trust is established
- "all life stages" language
- any phrasing that lets the adult-only scope slip

## Judgment
Strong enough to advance to the first-page decision.
This spine deliberately does not say "homepage" anywhere. The first-page decision step is where that call gets made.
