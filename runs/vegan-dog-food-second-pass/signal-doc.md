# Signal Doc — Second Pass

## What the product appears to be
A plant-based dry kibble for adult dogs, sold as a monthly subscription, positioned as a nutritionally-audited adult-maintenance food formulated by a board-certified veterinary nutritionist with third-party amino acid testing. Not a lifestyle brand, not therapeutic, not for puppies or medically restricted diets at launch.

## Likely target audience
- adult-dog owners who are vegan or vegetarian themselves
- specifically the subset who looked at plant-based dog food once and bounced off the soft evidence
- secondary (not served yet): puppy, senior or therapeutic-diet owners, and non-values-aligned curious owners

## Recurring pains or friction
- plant-based dog food marketing is heavier on ethics than nutrition
- "vet approved" and "complete and balanced" are used loosely in the category
- hard to tell which brands have taken nutritional adequacy seriously
- owners do not want a values-vs-nutrition tradeoff
- AAFCO compliance flavor (formulation vs. feeding trial) is almost never explained

## Desired outcomes
- confidence the food is nutritionally defensible for an adult dog
- specific, checkable claims instead of lifestyle language
- clarity about what the food is and is not for
- a brand that respects the trust question enough to answer it directly

## Trust questions or buying resistance
- is this actually nutritionally complete for my adult dog?
- who formulated it and what are their credentials?
- is compliance by formulation or by feeding trial?
- has the amino acid profile been verified or only formulated?
- what has the brand not done?
- is this appropriate for my dog's life stage or condition?

## Native phrases worth keeping
- formulated by a board-certified veterinary nutritionist
- AAFCO adult-maintenance nutrient profile
- third-party amino acid testing
- for adult dogs
- nutritional adequacy
- what we have done and what we have not done

## Generic language to avoid
- revolutionary pet nutrition
- optimize your dog's wellness
- sustainable nutrition for modern pet parents
- guilt-free feeding
- vet approved (bare)
- clinically proven
- healthiest food
- feel good about what you feed your dog

## What still feels unknown
- palatability experience
- whether consulting-nutritionist language will read as strong enough
- whether "adult only" reads as responsibility or limitation
- what mixed-life-stage households will do

## Weak claims to avoid early
- bare "vet approved"
- "complete nutrition for any dog"
- "healthier than conventional food"
- "most dogs thrive on Kind Bowl"
- "studies show" about studies not done
- outcome claims about individual dogs
- any therapeutic or preventative claim

## Judgment
Strong enough to advance. Materially narrower and more specific than the first-pass signal doc because the mechanism, proof and comparison briefs gave this pass something the first pass did not have.
