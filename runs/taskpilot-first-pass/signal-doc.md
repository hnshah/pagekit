## Current signal read

### What the product appears to be
TaskPilot appears to be a task and workflow product.
The name suggests help with prioritization, movement and execution.
At this stage, the object still feels underdefined, which makes it a good test of whether the process can reduce ambiguity instead of hiding it.

### Likely target audience(s)
Early plausible audiences:
- solo operators who juggle many moving tasks
- small teams that need clearer coordination
- founders or managers who feel work is slipping between tools

Confidence is low.
These are working audience hypotheses, not settled truths.

### Recurring pains or friction
Likely pain territory based on the product frame:
- tasks scatter across tools and lose momentum
- people lose track of what matters now
- coordination creates overhead
- planning feels organized on paper but breaks in execution

These are still category-level pains.
They need sharper source material before they can anchor strong homepage copy.

### Desired outcomes
Likely desired outcomes:
- clearer priorities
- less dropped work
- smoother handoffs
- more confidence that the right work is moving
- less time spent checking on status

### Trust questions or buying resistance
Likely early resistance:
- is this just another task app?
- what does it replace?
- is it for me or for my whole team?
- will it reduce work or add process?
- what makes it different from a task list with nicer language?

### Native vs generic language
More native starting words:
- keep work moving
- know what matters now
- fewer dropped balls
- clearer handoffs
- less status chasing

Generic words to avoid early:
- streamline productivity
- optimize workflow
- boost efficiency
- transform collaboration
- intelligent task management

### What still feels unknown
- what TaskPilot actually does in product terms
- what the sharpest first audience is
- what proof can be carried honestly
- whether the best wedge is personal clarity, team coordination or execution visibility
- what the strongest contrast is against existing tools

### Weak claims to avoid early
- all-in-one work operating system
- revolutionize your workflow
- never drop a task again
- the smartest way to manage work
- built for every team

## Current judgment
Usable first pass, but still too category-level.
The process is doing the right thing by exposing ambiguity.
We need at least one more stronger source artifact before the message spine step if we want the homepage draft to avoid generic task-software language.

## Likely failure modes
- defaulting into generic AI PM/copilot language
- vague productivity claims
- pretending we know the buyer more clearly than we do
- writing homepage copy before the message is reduced enough
