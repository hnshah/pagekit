# Proof Map — Vegan Dog Food Brand Homepage (Fresh Run)

## Page
- name: Homepage — nutrition-first opening
- audience: Values-led dog owners (vegan/vegetarian) and health-led dog owners (allergy/sensitivity cases)
- job: Build nutrition trust, answer the palatability objection, make the values case, convert to first order or trial

---

## Required proof by argument section

### Section 1 — Nutrition claim (hero)
**Proof needed:** A verifiable nutrition standard the product meets.
- Best case: AAFCO complete and balanced statement confirmed on packaging or product page
- Second best: Feeding trial results, veterinary nutritionist involvement in formulation
- Minimum: A clear statement of what standard is met and how the claim is backed

**Current gap:** AAFCO status unconfirmed for this run. The hero claim "your dog can thrive on this" requires this proof to be specific. If AAFCO complete-and-balanced cannot be claimed, the hero needs to be calibrated to what can actually be said.

**Risk if gap is not filled:** The hero claim floats — sounds like every other brand asserting its own nutritional quality. This is the highest-risk proof gap in the draft because it's in the first section. A vague nutrition claim in the hero will not earn the trust the rest of the page needs.

---

### Section 2 — How the nutrition is done
**Proof needed:**
- Protein source specifics: what plant proteins are used (peas, lentils, yeast, potato) and their amino acid profiles
- Amino acid completeness claim: is taurine added? L-carnitine? These questions are live in the dog food space and skeptical owners will ask
- Veterinary nutritionist involvement: if true, name it; if not, do not imply it
- Any third-party testing or certification

**Current gap:** Protein sources and amino acid handling unconfirmed. Draft will describe the formulation process generically and flag for product-specific fill-in.

**Risk:** If this section is vague ("carefully selected plant proteins"), it reads as marketing language rather than evidence. The section needs enough specificity to feel like a real answer, not a reassurance.

---

### Section 3 — Palatability
**Proof needed:** A real mechanism the brand offers if the dog won't eat it.
- Best case: Money-back guarantee stated plainly, or a sample/trial program
- Second best: A transition guide that reduces the "my dog won't eat it" failure rate
- Minimum: An honest statement of what the return/refund policy is

**Current gap:** Palatability guarantee or trial program unconfirmed. The draft will write this section as if a guarantee exists, with a verification flag. If no guarantee exists, the section needs to be rewritten to offer the next-best mechanism (transition guide, sample size option, etc.).

**Risk:** Without something real here, the section becomes a claim ("dogs love it") that owners have already heard and discounted. The section only does trust work if it offers a real fallback, not an assertion.

---

### Section 4 — Values
**Proof needed:** Factual accuracy about what the product does not contain and what the owner is no longer supporting by switching.
- What ingredients are excluded (factory-farmed meat, rendered byproducts)
- What sourcing practices are in place for the plant ingredients
- Any sustainability claims should be specific and verifiable, not general ("better for the planet")

**Current gap:** Sourcing specifics unconfirmed. The values section will stay at the level of what is plainly true (no animal-derived ingredients) without reaching for sustainability statistics that aren't verified.

**Risk:** Medium. This section doesn't need elaborate proof to work — the plain fact of what's excluded from the food is enough for most values-led owners. The risk is overclaiming on sustainability impact.

---

### Section 5 — Proof (owner stories)
**Proof needed:** Two or three owner stories with scenario-level specificity.

**Values-led owner story:**
- Scenario: Owner who is vegan/vegetarian, what they were worried about before switching, how the dog responded, what they observed after a period of use
- Needed: Real name, dog name or breed, enough specificity to be credible

**Health-led owner story:**
- Scenario: Dog with food allergies or sensitivities, what the problem was (itching, GI issues, ear infections), what changed on plant-based food, how long it took
- Needed: Real name, description of the health issue, observable improvement

**Current gap:** No real owner stories available for this run. Proof section will be written as placeholder structure with the format specified. Do not invent names, breeds, or outcomes.

**Risk:** High. Without real stories in this section, the page ends on a weaker note. This is acceptable for a first-pass draft but must be filled before publication. Generic testimonials ("great product, my dog loves it") will not do the proof work this section needs.

---

### Section 6 — CTA
**Proof needed:** None beyond what prior sections have earned.
**Risk:** If sections 1-5 have not done their work, the CTA is just a button. This section cannot recover upstream weakness.

---

## Weak spots — where the draft could drift into generic or unsupported language

**Weak spot 1: The hero nutrition claim**
Highest risk. If "your dog can thrive on this" is not backed by a specific, verifiable standard in the sub-headline, it sounds like every other brand's claim. The sub-headline must do real work: name the standard, name the certification, or name the formulation basis. "Nutritionally complete" alone is not enough.

**Weak spot 2: The formulation section**
Medium risk of drifting into "carefully selected plant-based proteins" language — which is the vegan dog food equivalent of "premium ingredients." The section needs to name the proteins, name the amino acid approach, and describe the formulation basis specifically enough that a skeptical owner feels they've learned something.

**Weak spot 3: The palatability section**
High risk of drifting into "dogs love it" or "most dogs transition easily." These are claims owners have already heard and discounted. The section only earns trust if it offers a real mechanism. Without one, consider cutting this section to a single honest sentence rather than a full section that overpromises.

**Weak spot 4: The values section**
Low-to-medium risk. The plain description of what the food doesn't contain ("no factory-farmed meat, no rendered byproducts") is self-evidently true and doesn't need elaborate backing. The risk is reaching for environmental impact statistics that aren't verified — these should be cut unless specific and sourced.

**Weak spot 5: Proof placeholders**
Same risk as in any run: if placeholder text is too polished, it may ship without being filled. All placeholders must be visually distinct and unambiguously marked as unfilled.
