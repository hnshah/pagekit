# Signal Doc — Vegan Dog Food Brand (Fresh Run)

## Product
A vegan dog food brand. Produces nutritionally complete, plant-based food for dogs. Positioned for owners who either want their dog's diet to align with their own values, or are looking for a meat-free option due to their dog's food sensitivities or allergies.

## Source basis
No primary interviews or transcripts. Signal drawn from:
- Amazon reviews of vegan dog food products (V-dog, Wild Earth, Halo Holistic, Benevo, Ami)
- Reddit discussions in r/vegan, r/dogs, r/veganpets
- Pet owner forums and vet Q&A sites
- Common objection language observed in vegan community discussions about pets
- Publicly observable brand copy and the gaps in it

Limitation: pattern-level signal, not verified from direct owner interviews. Downstream claims stay within what is repeatedly observed, not individually confirmed.

## Raw signal sources — repeated patterns

### Pains

**Values-led owners:**
- Feeling of dissonance: being vegan or vegetarian but feeding their dog chicken, beef, or fish every day
- Discomfort with the ingredients in conventional pet food — rendered byproducts, factory farmed meat, unnamed protein sources
- Wanting to extend their values to their whole household without worrying they're harming their dog

**Health-led owners:**
- Dog has been diagnosed with food allergies or sensitivities to common animal proteins (chicken is the most common allergen in dogs)
- Itching, skin problems, ear infections, GI issues traced to meat proteins
- Vet has suggested an elimination diet or novel protein source — plant-based becomes a candidate
- Tired of rotating through expensive limited-ingredient diets without resolution

**Shared:**
- Uncertainty about whether plant-based dog food is nutritionally complete — "I want to do this but I don't want to hurt my dog"
- Worry that their dog won't eat it — "my dog is picky" or "my dog has always eaten meat-based food"
- Not knowing which brands to trust — the vegan dog food market has a range of quality and the owner can't easily evaluate it

### Desires

**Values-led owners:**
- Feed their dog something they feel genuinely good about — not just "better than the worst option"
- Stop splitting their household ethics in two
- Find something that is nutritionally sound enough that they don't have to worry

**Health-led owners:**
- Find something that actually resolves the allergy or sensitivity issue
- Stop spending money on partial solutions
- Have something their dog will actually eat consistently

**Shared:**
- Confidence that the choice is nutritionally correct — not reassurance, actual evidence
- A dog that eats it happily and stays healthy on it
- Simplicity — one food that covers the full nutritional picture without supplements

### Objections
- "Dogs are omnivores but they evolved eating meat — this feels unnatural"
- "Plant-based protein isn't as bioavailable as animal protein for dogs"
- "My vet hasn't recommended this / hasn't heard of it / was skeptical"
- "My dog tried a plant-based food once and wouldn't eat it"
- "I've read that some vegan dog foods caused heart problems" (DCM concerns, though evidence is contested)
- "This is more expensive than regular food and I'm not sure it's worth it"

### Trust blockers
- **Nutritional completeness:** is this actually complete and balanced? What standard does it meet?
- **Palatability:** will my dog eat it? What happens if they won't?
- **Vet credibility:** is there any professional backing for this, or is this a wellness brand making its own claims?
- **Ingredient quality:** where do the plant proteins come from? How are they processed?
- **DCM concern:** there was a period of concern about grain-free and novel-protein diets and heart disease in dogs — some owners have this in the back of their minds

### Buying triggers
- Getting a dog for the first time as a vegan and wanting to start right
- Dog develops food allergies and conventional options aren't working
- Seeing another vegan pet owner mention a brand that worked for their dog
- Reading something about pet food ingredient sourcing and feeling disturbed enough to switch
- Vet suggests trying a novel protein source and plant-based comes up as an option

## Audience distinctions

### Values-led owners
- Already vegan or vegetarian themselves
- Primary question: "Can I do this without harming my dog?"
- Trust barrier is nutrition first — if they're satisfied on nutrition, the purchase follows easily
- Less resistant to the concept; resistant to the nutrition risk
- Will read ingredient lists and look for certifications
- More likely to pay a premium if trust is earned

### Health-led owners
- May not be vegan themselves — came to plant-based through their dog's medical history
- Primary question: "Will this actually fix my dog's allergy/sensitivity problem?"
- Trust barrier is efficacy first, then palatability
- More skeptical of brand claims, more likely to be looking for vet or community validation
- Will compare carefully and may try multiple options before committing

### Distinction that matters for copy
The values-led owner needs the nutrition argument to win; once won, the values argument is easy.
The health-led owner needs the efficacy argument to win; values are secondary or irrelevant.
The homepage must serve both without losing either. The nutrition-first argument order works for both: values-led owners accept the nutrition case quickly; health-led owners need it most.

## Candidate truths

1. **The nutrition question is real and needs a real answer.** "Plant-based diets can be nutritionally complete for dogs" is true at the category level, but it depends on formulation. The brand's job is to show that their formulation does the work — not to assert that plant-based is fine as a general claim.

2. **Palatability is a real barrier that most vegan dog food brands underweight.** Owner reviews consistently surface "my dog wouldn't eat it" as a reason for returning or stopping. The brand that addresses this directly — and backs it with a guarantee or a real claim — earns disproportionate trust.

3. **The dissonance is real but it can't open the argument.** Values-led owners feel the contradiction of feeding their dog factory-farmed meat. That feeling is real. But leading with it (before nutrition trust is built) reads as emotional pressure rather than an honest case. The values argument lands harder after the nutrition argument is won.

4. **Vet skepticism is a real obstacle that the brand can either address or leave to the customer.** Many owners say "my vet was skeptical." A brand that has vet endorsements or feeding trial data has a real advantage here. A brand without it needs to at least acknowledge the gap honestly.

---

## Open questions

- What AAFCO or equivalent standard does this product meet? (Complete and balanced vs. supplemental)
- Are there vet endorsements or veterinary nutritionist involvement in the formulation?
- What is the palatability story — is there a guarantee, a trial offer, or feeding data?
- What is the protein source — peas, lentils, yeast, potato? This matters for the DCM-adjacent objection.
- Is there a transition guide for switching dogs from meat-based food? (Important for reducing the churn at the "my dog won't eat it" point)
- Are there case studies or before/after allergy outcomes from health-led customers?
