# Working Log

## Run: vegan-dog-food-fresh-run
## Date: 2026-04-14

---

### Step 1 — Signal Doc
**Status:** complete

Signal drawn from publicly observable sources: Amazon reviews of vegan dog food products (V-dog, Wild Earth, Halo, Benevo), Reddit threads in r/vegan and r/dogs, pet owner forum discussions, vet Q&A sites, and common objection language in vegan pet owner communities.

Key finding: there are two meaningfully distinct audiences arriving at vegan dog food from different directions. Values-led owners (already vegan/vegetarian, feel dissonance feeding their dog meat) and health-led owners (dog has protein allergies or sensitivities, vet or research has pointed them toward plant-based options). These audiences share some trust barriers but their primary question is different. Signal doc holds both.

Also found: palatability is an underweighted trust barrier in most brand copy, but it surfaces constantly in owner reviews and forum discussion. "My dog won't eat it" is a real exit point that most vegan dog food homepages don't address directly.

---

### Step 2 — Message Spine
**Status:** complete

Three core truths held after reduction:
1. The nutrition question is real — and it deserves a real answer, not reassurance
2. Dogs can thrive on well-formulated plant-based diets — but "well-formulated" is doing a lot of work and needs to be shown, not asserted
3. Most dog food is made from ingredients most owners would reject if they saw them — the dissonance is real, but it can't be the opening move

Key spine decision: dropped "values without compromise" as the organizing frame. It leads with the emotional argument before the rational argument is made, which is backwards for an audience whose #1 trust barrier is "will this hurt my dog." Values language can appear downstream once nutrition trust is built.

---

### Step 3 — First-Page Decision
**Status:** complete

Decision: homepage, but with a non-standard opening.

The first move is not the values appeal — it's the nutrition claim stated plainly and then backed up. "Your dog can thrive on this" has to land before "and it aligns with what you believe" can add anything. If the nutrition trust isn't built first, the values appeal floats — it's nice to hear but not enough to buy.

Ruled out a dedicated nutrition science landing page as the first page: that page assumes the visitor is already sold on the concept and wants to go deeper. Most visitors are still at "is this even real?"

---

### Step 4 — Page Argument Shape
**Status:** complete

Argument order: recognition → nutrition claim (plain) → how the nutrition is done → palatability → values framing → proof → CTA.

The unusual move: values land in the middle, not at the top. The argument earns the right to make the values appeal by building nutrition and palatability trust first.

Named three sections as unnecessary in the first pass: ingredient glossary, sustainability stats in the hero, comparison table against meat-based brands.

---

### Step 5 — Proof Map
**Status:** complete

Main proof gaps: no AAFCO statement verified, no named vet endorsements, no feeding trial data, no palatability guarantee mechanism confirmed.

Draft constrained accordingly. Nutrition claims stay at the category level ("plant-based diets can be nutritionally complete for dogs") rather than product-specific claims that would require lab data to support. Palatability handled as a direct offer (guarantee language) rather than a statistic.

---

### Step 6 — Homepage Draft
**Status:** complete

Draft follows the approved argument shape. Nutrition first, palatability addressed directly, values framing in the middle, proof placeholders marked clearly. Two sections carry inline flags for product verification.

---

### Overall judgment
The fresh-start approach surfaced a meaningfully different argument structure from the prior run. Leading with nutrition rather than values changes the argument order significantly and may better serve the health-led audience who arrives skeptical of the concept rather than already aligned with the ethics.

Biggest remaining gap: palatability proof. If a money-back guarantee or a feeding trial result exists, Section 4 gets materially stronger. Without it, the draft can only assert confidence, not demonstrate it.

Upstream recommendation: owner interviews split by audience type (values-led vs. health-led) before publication. The two audiences may need different homepage variants or at least different hero treatments.
