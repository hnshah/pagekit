# First-Page Decision — Vegan Dog Food Brand (Fresh Run)

## Candidate first page
Homepage — with a nutrition-first opening, not a values-first opening.

## Why this page first
The homepage is where both audience types arrive first — values-led owners and health-led owners. Both audiences share the same primary trust barrier: "Is this nutritionally sound?" Neither can move forward without that question being answered. The homepage's job is to answer that question credibly and early, then let the rest of the argument follow.

The nutrition-first opening is the specific decision this run is testing against the prior pass. The hypothesis: a homepage that leads with "your dog can thrive on this, and here is why" earns more trust than one that leads with the values tension. Once nutrition trust is built, the values case is easy. The reverse is not true — a values appeal without nutrition backing produces resonance but not conversion.

## Page job
Help the visitor understand:
1. Dogs can genuinely thrive on a well-formulated plant-based diet — and this product is well-formulated
2. "Well-formulated" means something specific here: what standard is met, how protein completeness is handled
3. The palatability problem has been solved — their dog will eat this
4. The values argument: what they're no longer supporting by making this switch
5. What others in their situation have found

And help them do: buy or start a trial.

## Why this page before other pages
- A nutrition science page assumes the visitor is already past "is this real?" — they aren't yet
- An allergy/sensitivity page serves only the health-led audience; the homepage must serve both
- A values/mission page assumes nutrition trust is already built — it isn't
- A features/ingredients page is too detailed for a first visit

The homepage has to do the heavy lifting on nutrition trust before any other page can be useful. Nothing else can precede it.

## Visitor state on arrival
Two types:

**Values-led:** Arrives already believing plant-based dog food is a good idea in principle. Already aligned on the concept. Looking for a brand they can trust to do it right. Their question is "is this one safe/good?" not "is this possible?"

**Health-led:** Arrives because their dog has allergies or sensitivities and conventional food isn't working. May be skeptical of vegan food as a category. Their question is "could this actually help my dog?" They need to be convinced the nutrition is sound before the allergy benefit is relevant.

Both visitors have the nutrition question as their gatekeeping question. Answer it first.

## Main trust burden
Nutritional completeness. The owner needs to believe — with something more than confidence — that this food meets a real standard and won't harm their dog. This is the trust barrier that determines whether the rest of the page gets read.

Secondary trust burden: palatability. "My dog won't eat it" is the reason owners don't try, or try and return. The page has to address this directly and offer something real — a guarantee, a transition guide, a return policy — not just assert that dogs love it.

## Main proof burden
- A verifiable nutrition standard (AAFCO complete and balanced, feeding trial, veterinary nutritionist involvement)
- A palatability mechanism (guarantee, trial period, real owner reports with specificity)
- Owner examples that show a dog thriving — ideally with before/after health context for the health-led audience

## What this decision rules out for now
- Opening with sustainability or environmental impact data — belongs downstream in the page, not in the hero
- A hero section that leads with the values tension before nutrition is addressed
- A "how we compare to other vegan brands" section in the first pass — unnecessary complexity
- A dedicated team or founder story section — save for an About page
- Separate homepage variants by audience type — one page must serve both in this pass
