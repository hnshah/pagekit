# Signal Doc — Verdel

## Product
Verdel is grain-inclusive, plant-based dry kibble for adult dogs. AAFCO complete and balanced for adult maintenance. Primary protein: peas, lentils, chickpeas, nutritional yeast. Added taurine, L-carnitine, algae-derived DHA. Reviewed by Dr. Sarah Kim, DACVN before launch. 30-day money-back guarantee. $59 for 22 lb (approx. 30-day supply for a 40 lb dog). Online only.

## Source basis
Product brief (02-product-brief.md) is ground truth. Signal below draws on publicly observable owner discussion: Amazon reviews of vegan dog food products, Reddit (r/vegan, r/dogs, r/veganpets), vet forum Q&As, and prior PageKit vegan dog food runs in this repo.

## What the product appears to be
A credentialed plant-based dog food in a crowded and skeptic-heavy category. More nutritionally backed than most vegan dog foods (AAFCO + DACVN review). Grain-inclusive, which directly addresses the DCM concern that follows the category. The 30-day guarantee answers the palatability objection rather than ignoring it.

---

## Recurring pains and friction

**Values-led owners:**
- The daily dissonance of being vegan and feeding a dog factory-farmed meat
- Fear that switching means risking the dog's health to satisfy the owner's ethics
- Wanting something with a real nutritional basis, not just good intentions

**Health-led owners (allergy/sensitivity):**
- Dog has been on multiple limited-ingredient diets without resolution
- Vet suggested an elimination diet or novel protein; plant-based is a candidate
- Skeptical the protein sources will be sufficient
- Worried the dog won't eat something so different

**Shared:**
- Nutritional anxiety — "plant-based can't have everything a dog needs"
- DCM concern — the FDA grain-free/heart disease investigation has created ambient fear about legume-heavy diets even in grain-inclusive products
- Palatability — one bad experience with a previous vegan food is the most common reason for not trying again
- Vet skepticism — "my vet didn't recommend this"

---

## Desired outcomes
- Dog eats it, stays healthy, shows no decline in energy or coat quality
- Nutritional confidence — a basis beyond "I think this should be fine"
- Resolution of allergy symptoms without constant rotation (health-led audience)
- No more tension between what the owner believes and what the dog eats (values-led audience)

---

## Trust questions and buying resistance

| Question | What Verdel has to answer it |
|---|---|
| Is this nutritionally complete? | AAFCO adult maintenance + DACVN review |
| Is this grain-free — will this cause heart problems? | Explicitly grain-inclusive; oats and brown rice in the formula |
| What if my dog won't eat it? | 30-day money-back guarantee, no return required |
| Does my vet endorse this? | "Formulated with veterinary nutritionist review" — the honest claim |
| Is pea protein safe long-term? | Grain-inclusive vegan diets are not the category under investigation; the science is still live |

---

## Native language to keep
- "nutritionally complete" / "complete and balanced"
- "food sensitivity" / "protein allergy"
- "grain-inclusive" (important distinction from "grain-free")
- "30-day guarantee"
- "will my dog actually eat this"
- "I don't want to keep feeding factory-farmed meat"

## Language to drop
- "guilt-free" (condescending)
- "planet-friendly" (no certification)
- "vet approved" (formulated with DACVN review, not endorsed)
- "dogs love it" (no palatability rate data)
- "premium nutrition" (vague)
- "natural ingredients" (vague and legally slippery)

---

## Audience distinctions

**Values-led owners** already accept the concept. Their barrier is nutritional confidence. Once that is established, the purchase is easy. They need to see "complete and balanced" backed by something specific.

**Health-led owners** arrive skeptical of the category. They need the allergy/elimination diet case made specifically — why novel plant proteins may resolve a sensitivity to chicken or beef, and why the AAFCO standard still holds.

**DCM-concerned owners** cross both groups. Verdel's grain-inclusive formulation addresses this directly, but only if the page says "grain-inclusive" in a place they will read before they leave.
