# Page Argument Shape — Verdel Safety Landing Page

## Page
"Is Verdel safe for my dog?" — trust/safety landing page. Not a homepage.

## What the visitor needs to understand first
That this page will answer the safety question directly, with specifics — not with reassurance. The visitor arrived from a search about plant-based dog food safety. They are skeptical. The first section has to establish that what follows is factual and specific, not marketing.

## What needs to be earned next
Once the AAFCO claim lands: the grain-inclusive distinction. The DCM concern (grain-free, legume-heavy diets) is the ambient fear in this category. Stating "grain-inclusive" early — before a visitor decides the page is just another vegan dog food pitch — is the most important structural decision on this page.

After the grain-inclusive point: the DACVN review. What it means (a board-certified veterinary nutritionist reviewed the formulation) and what it does not mean (not "vet approved" in a clinical sense). The honest version earns more than the overstatement.

After the nutritional argument: the palatability question. The guarantee is the answer. State it plainly.

After trust is established: a brief section for the allergy/elimination diet visitor, because the signal doc shows they arrive here too and the page will serve them if it names their situation.

Then: how to buy (with price). No need for a full product page — a price, the guarantee reminder, and a CTA.

## Argument order

1. **The nutritional basis** — AAFCO adult maintenance, what that standard means, what Verdel does to meet it (protein sources, amino acid additions)
2. **The grain-inclusive fact** — what it is, why it matters for the DCM concern
3. **The DACVN review** — what it means and what it does not mean
4. **The guarantee** — 30-day, no return required; this is the palatability answer
5. **The allergy path** — brief: what plant proteins offer the food-sensitivity owner
6. **How to buy** — price, shipping, CTA

## Section plan

**Section 1 — Nutritional basis**
Heading: What Verdel is made of and what standard it meets.
Body: AAFCO adult maintenance statement. The three protein sources (peas, lentils, chickpeas, nutritional yeast). The amino acid additions (taurine, L-carnitine) and why they are added — plant proteins do not supply these in the ratios dogs require, so the formulation adds them. Algae-derived DHA. DACVN review is introduced here as the validation for the formulation decisions.
No brand story. No values appeal. Facts.

**Section 2 — Grain-inclusive**
Heading: Verdel contains oats and brown rice. It is not grain-free.
Body: One or two sentences on why this matters. The FDA's ongoing investigation into DCM involved grain-free, legume-heavy diets. Verdel's formulation is grain-inclusive specifically because the evidence points to whole grains as part of a complete diet. State this plainly, without overclaiming on the DCM science (which is still live).

**Section 3 — DACVN review**
Heading: The veterinary nutritionist review.
Body: Dr. Sarah Kim, DACVN reviewed the formulation before launch. What "DACVN" means (board-certified in veterinary nutrition). What the review covers (formulation adequacy, amino acid profile, macro balance). What it does not say — this is not an endorsement of plant-based diets in general, and Verdel does not claim "vet approved."

**Section 4 — The guarantee**
Heading: If your dog will not eat it, we refund your first bag.
Body: 30 days. No return required. The transition guide (10-day gradual mix-in) is in every box — because most dogs do adjust, and the guide helps. But if not, the refund stands.

**Section 5 — Food sensitivity path**
Heading: For dogs with food allergies or sensitivities.
Body: Dogs with confirmed or suspected sensitivities to chicken, beef, or other animal proteins may do well on a novel plant-based protein diet. Verdel uses peas, lentils, and chickpeas — proteins not found in conventional kibble. This is not a veterinary recommendation; owners who suspect food allergies should work with their vet on an elimination protocol.

**Section 6 — Buy**
Price and CTA only. $59 for 22 lb, free shipping, link to the product page.

## What sections would be unnecessary or harmful

**Brand story / founder section** — the visitor came for facts, not a founding narrative. Move to an About page.

**Values and ethics section** — the values argument is real but it is downstream of trust. A visitor who is not yet convinced the food is safe will not be moved by the ethics argument. If the trust is built, values can appear as a closing note on the product page, not here.

**Testimonials** — no real testimonials in the product brief. A placeholder testimonial section here would actively reduce credibility.

**Pricing comparison to other brands** — introduces competitive defensiveness before trust is established.

**"Learn more about plant-based nutrition" content block** — too deep for this page's job. Link to a nutrition page if one exists.

## Where trust should land
Sections 1 through 3, cumulatively. By the end of Section 3, the visitor should have enough factual basis to answer "is this safe?" in the affirmative. Section 4 (guarantee) handles the residual palatability concern.

## Where proof should land
Section 1 (AAFCO statement, amino acid additions) and Section 3 (DACVN review). These are the two verifiable proof assets available; they belong in the sections that need them, not in a separate proof section.

## Drafting constraints

### Proof and scope constraints
- No "vet approved" — the DACVN relationship is "formulated with veterinary nutritionist review"
- No palatability success rate or percentage — not in the product brief
- No DCM-safety claim beyond "grain-inclusive" — the research is still live
- No claim that Verdel resolves allergy symptoms — the allergy section names the mechanism (novel protein) and defers to veterinary guidance
- No external credential descriptions beyond what the product brief supports
- No sections not approved by this shape

### Anti-slop constraints (hard rules)
- No `Not X. Not Y.` heading or sentence pairs
- No `Not-X-but-Y` or `X-not-Y` rhetorical pivots unless they carry specific content the affirmation cannot
- No three parallel short sentences used for cadence (rule-of-three clustering)
- No sentence-start anaphora across three or more consecutive sentences
- No editorial voice narrating brand restraint, virtue or honesty
- No more than one em-dash per paragraph
- No `actually`, `additionally`, `testament`, `landscape`, `showcasing`, `genuinely`, `truly` as intensifiers
- No marketing-punchy one-liners as section enders
- No scare-quoted antithesis
- No copula-avoidance verbs (`serves as`, `boasts`, `features`, `showcases`, `delivers`, `empowers`)
- No signposting (`let's dive in`, `here's the thing`, `that's the deal`)
- No filler (`in order to`, `due to the fact that`)
- No significance inflation (`pivotal moment`, `critical juncture`, `the future of`)
