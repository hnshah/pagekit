# First-Page Decision — AI-First Project Management Tool (Chat Interface)

## Candidate first page
Homepage.

## Why this page first
The product sits in a crowded and fatigued category. Teams arriving at this page have likely tried two or three PM tools already and stopped using them. The homepage has to produce a specific recognition — "this understands why the other tools failed, not just that they did" — before any other page can be useful.

That recognition cannot happen on a features page, which assumes the visitor is past the "is this for me?" question. It cannot happen on an integrations page, which is for evaluation once interest is established. The homepage is where the framing argument lives.

## Page job
Help the team understand:
1. The reason their PM tool drifted was the maintenance burden, not the tool's quality — and this product addresses the maintenance burden directly
2. The AI does not assist with PM work; it does the PM work — a meaningfully different claim from what most AI PM tools offer
3. What the AI specifically does: what it reads, what it creates, how it handles being wrong
4. The interface is chat-first because that is where the information already is, not because it is a design choice

And help them do: start a trial or request access.

## Why this page before other pages
A "why chat-first" page would reach teams already sold on the concept — it has no argument to make for teams who are skeptical. A migration or integrations page is premature before the core claim lands. A pricing page is useless before trust is built.

One alternative considered and rejected: a landing page targeted specifically at post-Jira or post-Linear teams, with the headline directly addressing PM tool abandonment. Rejected because it narrows the audience before the homepage has established the product. The homepage makes the same argument without narrowing.

## Visitor state on arrival
Three likely arrival states:

**The skeptical engineer:** Has heard "AI PM tool" before, has low prior that this will be different, is scanning for a reason to dismiss it quickly. Needs the maintenance-burden framing to land in the first section, and the accuracy section later to stay. Will leave if the page reads like category marketing.

**The PM under water:** Is currently doing too much manual update work. Arrives with more openness but a higher accuracy threshold — they are reporting on this data and need to believe the AI is reliable enough to trust for stakeholder reviews.

**The founder or team lead pre-chaos:** Has felt the coordination start to slip as the team has grown. Is looking for something before it breaks, not after. Has less PM tool fatigue than the others and is more open to trying something new, but needs to understand the specific mechanism.

## Main trust burden
AI accuracy: what happens when the AI gets something wrong? This is the gatekeeping question for engineering teams in particular. The homepage cannot bury it in an FAQ. It needs a section that describes the correction model honestly — not "the AI is accurate" but "when the AI misses something, here is what happens."

## Main proof burden
Mechanism specificity: what does the AI actually extract from a conversation? The claim "AI maintains your project structure" is vague enough to be discounted. The proof burden is showing the specific behaviors — tasks created from a particular type of message, status updates triggered by particular language, blockers surfaced from a thread where someone said "waiting on X."

Secondary proof burden: a real team case showing the board reflecting reality without a manual update pass.

## What this decision rules out for now
- A feature matrix comparing this to Jira, Linear, or Asana — creates defensive positioning before the primary claim is made
- Security or compliance credentials in the homepage body — relevant for enterprise evaluation, not for first contact with a small team
- Pricing in this pass — premature before the core trust work is done
- A team or founder story section — save for an About page; does not do trust work for a skeptical engineering visitor
