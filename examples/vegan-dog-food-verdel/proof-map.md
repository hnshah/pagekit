## Proof by section

### Section 1 — Nutritional basis
**What can be said:** AAFCO adult maintenance. Protein sources (peas, lentils, chickpeas, nutritional yeast). Taurine and L-carnitine added because plant proteins do not supply them in the same ratios as animal proteins — this is a factual statement of nutritional chemistry, not a claim. Algae-derived DHA. DACVN review.
**What cannot be said:** Any claim that the food "provides everything a dog needs" beyond the AAFCO standard. No health-outcome claims. "Complete protein" without qualification — the formulation adds taurine and L-carnitine precisely because plant proteins are not complete in the animal-protein sense.
**Gap:** No feeding trial data — the AAFCO standard here is nutrient-profile verification, not a feeding trial. The page should not imply a feeding trial was conducted.

### Section 2 — Grain-inclusive
**What can be said:** Verdel contains oats and brown rice. It is not grain-free. The FDA's investigation into DCM involved grain-free, legume-heavy diets. Verdel's formulation is grain-inclusive.
**What cannot be said:** That grain-inclusive formulation definitively prevents DCM — the science is still live. The page can say "grain-inclusive" and note the FDA context without claiming a safety outcome.
**Gap:** No DCM-specific study data for Verdel. The claim stays at the formulation level.

### Section 3 — DACVN review
**What can be said:** Dr. Sarah Kim holds a DACVN credential (board-certified in veterinary nutrition). She reviewed the formulation before launch. This covered formulation adequacy and the amino acid profile.
**What cannot be said:** "Vet approved." Verdel does not carry that claim. The review is a formulation review, not a clinical endorsement of plant-based diets for dogs generally.
**Gap:** The scope of the review (what specifically Dr. Kim evaluated) is not specified in the product brief beyond "reviewed before launch." The page should describe this at the level the brief supports.

### Section 4 — The guarantee
**What can be said:** 30-day money-back guarantee. No return required. Refund on first bag.
**What cannot be said:** A palatability rate or "X% of dogs accept the transition." No such data exists in the brief.
**Gap:** None for this section — the guarantee is the complete answer available.

### Section 5 — Food sensitivity path
**What can be said:** Dogs with sensitivities to animal proteins may do well on a novel plant-based protein diet. Verdel uses peas, lentils, and chickpeas — proteins not present in conventional kibble. Owners who suspect food allergies should work with their vet.
**What cannot be said:** That Verdel treats or resolves food allergies. The page names the mechanism and defers to veterinary guidance.
**Gap:** No case studies or testimonials from allergy owners in the product brief.

### Section 6 — Buy
**What can be said:** $59 / 22 lb, free shipping on orders $40+, ships in 2 business days.
**What cannot be said:** Anything not in the brief.

## Weak spots

**Weak spot 1: Section 1 — "complete protein" language**
High risk of drifting into "complete protein" phrasing that overstates what plant proteins provide. The honest version — "plant proteins do not supply taurine and L-carnitine in the same ratios as animal proteins, so the formulation adds them" — is both accurate and more credible than a simpler claim.

**Weak spot 2: Section 2 — DCM overclaim**
Medium-high risk. Stating "grain-inclusive" is safe. Implying that grain-inclusive means DCM-safe is not. The science is still live; the page should state the formulation fact without asserting a safety outcome.

**Weak spot 3: Section 3 — DACVN scope creep**
Medium risk. The page may drift toward "vet reviewed" or "vet approved" language. The draft must hold the line at "formulated with veterinary nutritionist review" — no more, no less.

**Weak spot 4: Section 5 — allergy claim creep**
Medium risk. "May help dogs with food allergies" is the acceptable framing. "Resolves food allergies" or "designed for allergy dogs" overstates. The brief does not support an allergy-specific product claim.
