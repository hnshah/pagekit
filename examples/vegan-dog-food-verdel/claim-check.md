# Claim Check

## Severity
Hard. This is a health-adjacent product (dog food) with a nutritional claim (AAFCO), a clinical-credential claim (DACVN), and a category-level safety concern (DCM). Any clinical drift, unsourced modifier, or credential scope creep must be flagged.

---

## Flags

### Flag 1
**Line (Section 1):** "That is the same standard used by conventional adult dog food — not a reduced bar for plant-based products."
**Type:** Vague claim / potential editorial voice
**Issue:** "Not a reduced bar" narrates a comparison the reader may not have made. It pre-argues against a concern rather than stating the fact.
**Rewrite:** Remove the parenthetical. "Verdel meets AAFCO complete-and-balanced nutritional profiles for adult maintenance. That is the standard used by conventional adult dog food." The comparison is implicit and the reader can draw it; stating it creates the impression the brand is defending itself.

---

### Flag 2
**Line (Section 1):** "Plant proteins differ from animal proteins in amino acid ratios: they do not supply taurine or L-carnitine in the quantities adult dogs require."
**Type:** Unsourced clinical statement — acceptable at hard severity only if grounded.
**Issue:** This is a factual claim about nutritional chemistry. It is accurate at the category level and supported by established veterinary nutrition literature. Confirmed by product brief (Verdel adds taurine and L-carnitine specifically). The claim is defensible; the draft adds the supplement fact immediately after, which backs it.
**Decision:** Keep. The supplement fact in the same sentence grounds the claim in product reality.

---

### Flag 3
**Line (Section 2):** "Verdel's formulation is grain-inclusive. The oats and brown rice are in the recipe specifically because whole grains are part of a complete adult diet, and because the available evidence points toward grain-inclusive diets as the sounder choice."
**Type:** Outrun claim — "available evidence points toward grain-inclusive diets as the sounder choice"
**Issue:** This reaches beyond what the product brief supports. The product brief says Verdel is grain-inclusive; it does not cite a specific evidence base. "The available evidence points toward" is a scientific claim the brief does not back.
**Rewrite:** "Verdel's formulation is grain-inclusive — oats and brown rice are in the recipe. It is not grain-free." State the fact; omit the evidence-base claim the brief cannot support.

---

### Flag 4
**Line (Section 2):** "The DCM research is still active. What Verdel can say is what the formulation contains, not what it prevents."
**Type:** Editorial voice narrating brand restraint
**Issue:** The brand is announcing its own honesty rather than demonstrating it. If the prior sentence already said "grain-inclusive" and refused to claim a prevention outcome, this sentence is redundant meta-commentary.
**Rewrite:** Cut entirely. The prior sentence already holds the line; this one narrates that it is doing so.

---

### Flag 5
**Line (Section 3):** "The review covered the amino acid profile, macro ratios, and alignment with AAFCO adult maintenance profiles."
**Type:** Unsourced scope claim — minor at normal severity, flagged at hard
**Issue:** The product brief says the formulation was "reviewed by Dr. Sarah Kim, DACVN before launch" — it does not specify what the review scope included. The draft lists specific review areas the brief does not enumerate.
**Rewrite:** "The review covered formulation adequacy against the AAFCO standard." Stay at the level the brief supports; do not invent scope.

---

### Flag 6
**Line (Section 3):** "This is a formulation review, not a feeding trial. Verdel is sold as meeting AAFCO nutrient profiles, not as a diet validated through a feeding trial. That is the honest scope of the claim."
**Type:** Editorial voice narrating brand honesty — "That is the honest scope of the claim."
**Issue:** The first two sentences do the work. The third sentence narrates the brand's honesty rather than trusting the reader to see it.
**Rewrite:** Cut the final sentence. End at "not as a diet validated through a feeding trial."

---

### Flag 7
**Line (Section 4):** "Most dogs adjust within the transition period."
**Type:** Unsourced quantitative modifier — flagged at hard severity
**Issue:** "Most dogs" is a quantity claim. No palatability data is in the product brief. The guarantee is the honest answer to palatability; this sentence adds a claim the brief does not support.
**Rewrite:** Cut. The transition guide and the guarantee do the work; the "most dogs" claim adds nothing the guarantee has not already answered.

---

## Summary

7 flags. 3 rewrites (flags 1, 3, 4 merged into section changes). 2 cuts (flags 4, 6, 7). 1 decision to keep (flag 2).

No lines require upstream repair to the proof map — all issues are scope-creep or editorial voice in the draft, not gaps in the proof map itself.
