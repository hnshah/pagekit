# Evaluation

## Logging level
Fully logged per `frameworks/run-logging.md`.

## What worked

- The first-page decision produced a genuinely different page from both prior vegan dog food runs. Choosing the safety landing page over a homepage changed the argument structure substantially: no brand narrative, no values section, no testimonials — just the trust questions answered in order.
- Committing to a product name (Verdel) and concrete facts in the product brief eliminated placeholder hedging throughout. Every factual claim in the draft traces to a specific brief line.
- The grain-inclusive distinction earned its own section. Prior runs mentioned it; this run treated it as a first-class argument rather than a footnote.
- The claim-check at hard severity caught 7 issues: scope-creep in the DACVN review description, an outrun evidence-base claim in Section 2, editorial voice in two places, an unsourced "most dogs" modifier, and a defensive framing in the AAFCO statement. The corrected draft is tighter on every one of these.
- Slop-check caught one em-dash stacking issue in the draft before the claim-check pass.

## What felt weak

- Section 5 (food sensitivity path) is the thinnest section. It names the mechanism (novel proteins) and defers to veterinary guidance, which is the honest position — but it is short enough that it may feel like an afterthought for the health-led owner who arrived specifically for this information. A dedicated allergy landing page would serve this audience better; for now the section does its job without overclaiming.
- The DACVN review scope is constrained by the product brief: "reviewed before launch" is all the brief says. The draft cannot enumerate what the review covered beyond "formulation adequacy against the AAFCO standard." If the actual scope of Dr. Kim's review is more specific, that detail would strengthen this section considerably.

## What stayed thin

- No testimonials or owner stories — the product brief does not provide any, and the run did not invent them. The corrected draft ends without social proof, which is a real gap for conversion. The first real owner stories should be added as proof once available.
- No palatability data beyond the guarantee. The draft uses the guarantee as the answer to palatability — which is the honest move — but a single owner quote describing a successful transition would strengthen Section 4 without requiring statistics.

## Where outputs drifted generic

- Section 5 risks reading as boilerplate "consult your vet" copy. The first sentence about novel proteins is specific; the second sentence is standard disclaimers. Acceptable for this pass, but the section would benefit from a real allergy owner scenario if one became available.

## Final run judgment

The run holds. The first-page decision is the main structural contribution: a trust/safety page built entirely around answering the safety question, rather than a third homepage variant. The claim-check produced meaningful corrections — not cosmetic rewrites. The draft is honest about what the product can and cannot claim. Ready for publication once testimonials are available and the DACVN review scope is confirmed in more detail.
