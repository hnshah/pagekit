# First-Page Decision — Verdel

## Candidates considered

**Candidate A: Homepage**
Standard brand entry point. Works for visitors arriving via brand search or referral. Needs to serve multiple audience types at once. Both prior vegan dog food runs in this repo produced homepage drafts — one values-first, one nutrition-first.

**Candidate B: "Is plant-based food safe for my dog?" — trust/safety landing page**
High search-intent match. The most common concern a new visitor has is safety, not brand discovery. A page structured entirely around answering that question (AAFCO standard, grain-inclusive, DACVN review, guarantee) could convert better for the organic search audience and reduce the trust gap before the brand argument starts. This is the non-homepage option the message spine flagged for serious evaluation.

**Candidate C: Allergy and food sensitivity landing page**
Serves the health-led audience specifically. Explains why novel plant proteins can resolve a chicken or beef sensitivity. High conversion potential for this specific audience; too narrow to serve as the only first page.

**Candidate D: Nutrition science deep-dive page**
Too deep for first contact. Assumes the visitor already accepts the concept and wants to go further. Not a first page.

## Decision

**Candidate B: "Is Verdel safe for my dog?" — trust/safety landing page.**

### Why this page first

The message spine's three truths (AAFCO basis, grain-inclusive, guarantee) are all trust arguments. None of them are brand-awareness arguments. A visitor arriving at a homepage for an unknown brand and an unfamiliar category needs the trust question answered before brand positioning can land. The homepage model distributes attention across brand introduction, product description, values, and proof — which dilutes the trust argument by surrounding it with other content.

A page built entirely around the safety question concentrates the argument. The visitor arrives asking "is this safe?" and the page answers that question directly, specifically, and in order of what matters to them. The brand introduction is a byproduct of earning that trust, not the opening move.

Two prior runs used homepage. This run tests whether a trust-first page structure — stripped of brand narrative — converts the skeptical new visitor better.

### Page job

Help the visitor answer: is Verdel nutritionally complete, and is it safe to feed my adult dog?

Specifically:
1. What nutritional standard it meets (AAFCO adult maintenance, stated plainly)
2. Why grain-inclusive matters for the DCM concern (early, not buried)
3. What the DACVN review means and does not mean
4. What happens if the dog will not eat it (30-day guarantee)
5. What the allergy/elimination path looks like (brief, for health-led visitors)

### Visitor state on arrival

Searching for "is vegan dog food safe for dogs," "plant-based dog food AAFCO," or "vegan dog food without grain-free problems." Has a specific concern, not a general shopping intent. Arrives skeptical, not curious.

### Main trust burden

Nutritional completeness and grain-inclusive status. Both must appear in the first section, not after a brand introduction.

### Main proof burden

AAFCO adult maintenance + DACVN review are the proof assets available. The guarantee is the proof for palatability. No testimonials available yet — the page cannot fake this.

### What this rules out for now

- A hero that leads with brand name and tagline
- A values/ethics section in the first half
- Pricing in this page — the trust question comes before the purchase decision
- Competitor comparison content
