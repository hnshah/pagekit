## Model
claude-sonnet-4-6 (Claude Code agentic session)

## Raw output (as produced)

### What the visitor needs to understand first

That the problem they've been experiencing — going dark on a relationship that matters, reaching out without knowing where they left things — has a name and is a pattern, not a personal failure. The page must produce recognition before it offers a solution.

### What must be earned next

The product distinction. After recognition, the visitor's next question is "why isn't my current system enough?" The page must show specifically what fails about the current alternatives (inbox, spreadsheet, sales CRM) without claiming to be a "CRM" — which would trigger comparison-shopping before the visitor has committed to the problem.

### What must be earned after that

The maintenance concern. After the visitor has recognized the problem and accepted that their current tools are wrong for it, the question becomes: "Will I actually use this?" The page must address adoption doubt directly — not with generic claims about ease of use, but with the specific features that reduce friction (weekly digest, iOS app for quick capture) and the zero-lock-in posture (data export, no credit card for trial).

### Argument order

1. Name the failure mode (recognition section)
2. Show what current tools fail to track (why-the-gap section)
3. Introduce what Me CRM tracks (product section — what it is, specifically, not what category it is)
4. Address maintenance doubt (adoption section)
5. The trust floor: data handling, trial, export
6. CTA

### Sections this page needs

**Section 1 — The failure mode (hero)**
Names the experience: you meant to follow up, you don't remember where you left things, the relationship has quietly drifted. No product name in the first sentence. Recognition before solution.

**Section 2 — Why your current system doesn't hold this**
Specific about what breaks: email threads scatter context, spreadsheets require deliberate update effort that disappears under pressure, sales CRMs track deals not relationship state. This section is not "compare us to competitors" — it is "name the structural reason the problem persists."

**Section 3 — What Me CRM tracks**
Introduces the product through the state card concept: last contact, what was discussed, what you owe, next step. Shows the weekly digest and reminder as the mechanism that prevents relationships going dark. Specific features, not capabilities language.

**Section 4 — The maintenance question, answered**
Addresses adoption doubt head-on: the weekly digest surfaces what needs attention without requiring the founder to remember to log in; the iOS app captures notes immediately after a conversation before context fades. Honest about what still requires effort (initial setup, consistent note-taking). Does not overclaim.

**Section 5 — Trust floor**
Data: contact data not sold, data export always available. Trial: 14 days, no credit card required. Pricing: free tier (25 relationships), Solo $19/month. Brief — trust floor, not a full pricing page.

**Section 6 — CTA**
Start the trial. One action. Reinforce the no-credit-card-required point.

### Sections this page does not need

- A features grid or capabilities matrix: this is not a product features page
- A testimonials section: none available; placeholder testimonials would undermine the honest posture of the page
- An integrations section: the Gmail import is worth naming in Section 3, but a full integrations list is a downstream page
- A "for teams" section: the team plan exists but is not the primary audience for this page; a founder-specific first page should not fragment between solo and team paths
- A competitor comparison table: the argument from distinction should be experiential ("here is what fails"), not comparative ("here is how we rank")
- A founder story section: the origin story earns trust downstream; it should not lead before the user's problem

### Where trust should land

Primary trust is in the problem description itself — precision of naming earns credibility. Secondary trust is the trial structure (no credit card) and the data posture (no selling, export always available). These belong near the CTA, after the argument has been made.

### Where proof should land

Section 3 (product section): the relationship state card is the proof that the product actually does what it claims. Section 4 (maintenance section): the weekly digest and iOS app are proof that the adoption problem has a specific mechanism, not a general promise. The 200-waitlist number can appear in Section 5 as a signal of early validation — not as a proof of usage.

### Drafting constraints

**Proof and scope constraints:**
- no bare "relationship intelligence" or outcome claims ("increase funding probability," "close deals faster") — no data to support these
- no external-credential descriptions beyond what the brief supports
- no clinical, legal or regulatory-adjacent phrasing
- no outrun mechanism or evidence-base claims
- no sections not approved by this shape

**Anti-slop constraints (hard rules):**
- no "Not X. Not Y." heading or sentence pairs
- no "Not-X-but-Y" or "X-not-Y" rhetorical pivots unless they carry specific content the affirmation cannot
- no three parallel short sentences used for cadence (rule-of-three clustering)
- no sentence-start anaphora across three or more consecutive sentences
- no editorial voice narrating brand restraint, virtue or honesty
- no em-dashes in body copy (use a colon, comma, or parentheses instead)
- no "actually", "additionally", "testament", "landscape", "showcasing", "genuinely", "truly" as intensifiers
- no marketing-punchy one-liners as section enders
- no scare-quoted antithesis
- no copula-avoidance verbs ("serves as", "boasts", "features", "showcases", "delivers", "empowers")
- no signposting ("let's dive in", "here's the thing", "that's the deal")
- no filler ("in order to", "due to the fact that")
- no significance inflation ("pivotal moment", "critical juncture", "the future of")

**Inline verification flag convention:**
When a product-specific claim has not been verified against briefs or ground truth, mark it inline: *[verification flag: confirm X against Y before publishing]*
