# Claim Check — Me CRM (Problem-First Landing Page)

## Page
Me CRM — Problem-First Landing Page

## Draft version reviewed
`runs/personal-crm-founders/first-page-draft.md` (step 06, pre slop-check)

## Severity
**Normal.** The draft is pre-launch with no user outcome data or testimonials. Normal severity flags the full claim-type list but tolerates mild editorial voice and common-usage quantitative modifiers where the underlying observation is a reasonable pattern-level claim. Chosen because: the copy is already careful, the proof map is explicit about what is and is not defensible, and a hard pass would flag borderline modifiers in category-level descriptions that are not product claims.

## Claims reviewed

---

### Claim 1

> "Relationship state, not contact volume."

**Type:** ai-slop tell — negation pattern (X-not-Y)

**Why this is weak:** The heading reaches for sharpness through negation rather than through what the product does. "Contact volume" is a straw idea; no tool Me CRM is competing with pitches itself on contact volume. The negation does no work the affirmation cannot do more directly.

**Proof status:** Not a proof issue (slop tell only)

**Proposed correction:** Drop the contrast. Name the thing the section actually delivers.

> "What Me CRM tracks for each relationship"

Or keep the section heading as the structural label it already is and let the body carry the meaning.

---

### Claim 2

> "Spreadsheets work until you stop updating them. That usually happens three weeks in, when things get busy and the deliberate effort the spreadsheet requires disappears under everything else."

**Type:** Unsourced quantitative modifier — "usually," "three weeks in"

**Why this is weak:** "Usually" is doing a frequency claim. "Three weeks in" is doing a precision claim. Neither is sourced. The proof map logs this as a pattern-level observation accurate to the founder experience — but it does not commit to a frequency or a timeline. At normal severity this matters because "three weeks in" sounds like data when it is a rhetorical estimate.

**Proof status:** Supportable with existing source material if the product brief includes founder research; not currently committed to in the proof map.

**Proposed correction:** Keep the observation; remove the unsourced precision.

> "Spreadsheets work until you stop updating them. That tends to happen when things get busy and the deliberate effort the spreadsheet requires runs out of time."

Or, if "three weeks" comes from real founder research, add it to the proof map and restore the line with a verification flag.

---

### Claim 3

> "Me CRM is built for the 50–100 relationships that most determine where your company goes."

**Type:** Unsourced quantitative modifier — "most determine"; borderline vague claim

**Why this is weak:** "Most determine" is a ranking claim without a source. The 50–100 range is also asserted without grounding — it is a product design choice, not a research finding. Together, the sentence implies Me CRM has determined which relationships matter most and how many there are, when what the product brief actually says is that it is designed for a focused set of relationships (the product limits free accounts to 25 and positions itself against high-volume CRMs).

**Proof status:** Supportable with existing source material if the 50–100 range is a stated product design parameter; not currently committed to in the proof map.

**Proposed correction:** Anchor the range in the product's design rather than in a claim about what determines company outcomes.

> "Me CRM is built for a focused set of relationships — the investors, advisors, early customers, and connectors your company depends on most."

If 50–100 is an explicit product design parameter stated in the brief, restore the range with a verification flag and move the claim to feature-description language: "designed for up to 100 relationships."

---

### Claim 4

> "A quick note captured then is worth more than a detailed log you try to reconstruct later."

**Type:** Vague claim / unsupported claim

**Why this is weak:** "Worth more" is a value comparison that sounds like a product insight but is unverifiable as stated. The proof map lists the iOS app as a feature for immediate post-conversation note capture — it does not commit to claims about comparative note quality. The line is doing outcome language under the cover of folk wisdom.

**Proof status:** Not yet supportable — the proof map does not commit to outcome comparisons of this kind.

**Proposed correction:** Replace the comparison with what the feature actually does.

> "The iOS app is for the two minutes after a meeting. A note captured immediately stays tied to the conversation; a note reconstructed later loses the detail that makes it useful."

Or simply:

> "The iOS app is for the two minutes after a meeting. Capture the note before the details fade."

---

### Claim 5

> "The lower the friction of the entry point, the more likely the record stays current."

**Type:** Outrun claim — implies a usage outcome the proof map explicitly flags

**Why this is weak:** The proof map explicitly lists "outcome claims ('follow up more consistently,' 'keep relationships warmer')" as risky moves to avoid for this section. "The more likely the record stays current" is an outcome claim in the same family. The product cannot yet demonstrate that lower friction entry points translate to higher record currency in Me CRM users, because there are no Me CRM users yet (pre-launch).

**Proof status:** Not yet supportable — no usage data exists.

**Proposed correction:** Replace the outcome claim with the design rationale.

> "The iOS app is designed for that moment — a field for the note, and a tap to attach it to the right relationship."

---

### Claim 6

> "What it does is hold the state between conversations so the work you do compounds instead of starting from scratch each time."

**Type:** Outrun claim — outcome language the proof map flags

**Why this is weak:** "The work you do compounds" is an outcome claim. The proof map lists "Any sentence implying Me CRM users have better outcomes" as explicitly flagged. The product's honest claim is that it holds relationship state — what users do with that state is theirs. "Compounds" smuggles in a growth narrative the product cannot yet support.

**Proof status:** Not yet supportable — no outcome data exists.

**Proposed correction:** Describe what the product holds, not what it produces.

> "What it does is hold the state between conversations so you are not starting from scratch each time you reach out."

---

### Claim 7

> "Me CRM has no pipeline, no deal stages, and no lead scoring. Those are not missing features. They are the wrong tool for this job."

**Type:** ai-slop tell — marketing-punchy section ender

**Why this is weak:** The first sentence is precise and useful. The second and third sentences exist to feel quotable. "Those are not missing features. They are the wrong tool for this job." is a two-beat rhetorical punch. It adds no concrete information; the reader already understood the point from the first sentence and the paragraphs before it. The anti-slop framework flags this pattern directly: "sections should end on the concrete thing the reader should do or understand."

**Proof status:** Not a proof issue (slop tell only)

**Proposed correction:** Cut the last two sentences. The first sentence closes the section cleanly.

> "Me CRM has no pipeline, no deal stages, and no lead scoring."

## Summary

### Strongest remaining risks

- **Outcome claims in Section 4** (flags 5 and 6): These are the most likely lines to undermine the page's credibility if a skeptical reader notices them. A pre-launch product cannot back a compounding-work or record-currency claim. The fix is to stop one step earlier: describe the feature design, not the user result.

- **Unsourced precision in Section 2** (flag 2): "Three weeks in" is the only line in the draft that reads as data but is not. Readers who have experienced spreadsheet CRM abandonment will recognize the truth of the observation — but "three weeks" specifically will feel like a number someone made up. Remove the precision or source it.

### Recurring patterns on this draft

**Outcome language (flags 5 and 6):** Two lines in Section 4 cross from feature description into implied user outcome. Both use the same move: describe a design choice, then pivot to "therefore X will happen for you." The pivot is the problem. This pattern is worth absorbing into the page-argument-shape drafting constraints for this product as an explicit rule: *for pre-launch products without usage data, end each feature description at the design choice, not the expected result.*

**Negation / contrast constructions (flags 1 and 7):** Two places reach for rhetorical sharpness through negation rather than through description. The Section 3 heading uses X-not-Y; Section 3 body close uses a two-sentence rhetorical punch. Both are slop tells. One instance would be tolerable at normal severity; two in the same section signals the draft has a habit of reaching for contrast as a closer.

### Recurring slop patterns on this draft

- **Marketing-punchy section ender** (anti-slop pattern 7): Fired once explicitly (flag 7), and the X-not-Y heading (flag 1) is structurally related.
- **Negation patterns** (anti-slop pattern 1, specifically X-not-Y): Fired in the Section 3 heading and implicitly in the two-sentence ender.

### Where proof is the real gap

The proof map is clear: no outcome data exists. Flags 5 and 6 are proof gaps, not voice gaps. The copy arrived at outcome language because the section (maintenance question) naturally ends by asking "so will this work?" — and the draft reached for an answer the product cannot yet give. The proof map should flag this section type as outcome-adjacent and the page argument shape should specify: *for pre-launch builds, the maintenance section should end on mechanism, not result.*

### Upstream artifact that needs repair

- **Proof map** — add an explicit note that Section 4 (maintenance) is outcome-adjacent; the section should describe the digest and app mechanisms and stop before predicting user behavior.
- **Page argument shape drafting constraints** — add: no outcome claims in the maintenance section for pre-launch products; end on mechanism.
- **Anti-slop framework** — the negation-as-closer pattern (two-sentence rhetorical punch at section end) is worth naming explicitly as a variant of the marketing-punchy ender.
