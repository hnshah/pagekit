# Evaluation

## Logging level
Fully logged per `frameworks/run-logging.md`.

## What worked

- The first-page decision held. The problem-first landing page structure forced recognition to come before the product name — which is the right order for an audience that doesn't yet know the problem is solvable. A homepage would have surfaced the category label ("CRM") too early and triggered comparison-shopping before the visitor had acknowledged the problem.
- Step-03 now requires a paragraph FOR each candidate before any rejection. That constraint surfaced a genuine close call: the category-explainer (Candidate C) has a real argument for founders who have already cycled through Salesforce or Notion. That sub-segment is worth a follow-up page; naming it explicitly in the decision is useful.
- Section 4 (the maintenance question) is the most differentiated section. Naming the adoption concern directly rather than burying it is the honest move and the structurally correct one — it's the question the skeptical founder is already asking.
- The proof map was explicit about the pre-launch constraint. That explicitness caught the outcome language in Section 4 at claim-check (flags 5 and 6) rather than letting it publish.
- Claim-check at normal severity caught 7 flags: 2 ai-slop tells, 2 unsourced quantitative modifiers, 1 vague claim, 2 outrun claims. All resolved in the corrected draft.

## What felt weak

- Section 4 needed the most correction. Two consecutive rewrites produced a structural seam (duplicate sentence starts) that the subagent caught and repaired. The draft's instinct to end on a result rather than a mechanism is a consistent pull — the proof map constraint should be more explicit upstream.
- The Section 2 spreadsheet observation ("three weeks in") was a precision estimate dressed as data. The corrected version is less vivid but more honest.

## What stayed thin

- No social proof at all. The 200-waitlist number is the only trust signal beyond product features. Section 5 (trust floor) is carrying a disproportionate load without testimonials or any usage narrative.
- The maintenance section (Section 4) works as a mechanism description, but it cannot close the adoption concern — that requires either testimonials ("here is how I actually use it") or a free tier structure that lets the founder prove it to themselves before committing.

## Where outputs drifted generic

- Section 2 ("email threads, spreadsheets, and sales CRMs all fail at the same thing") is the most category-default section. The observations are accurate but they would appear, in roughly this form, on any founder-CRM product page. The section does its structural job (explains the gap) without adding Me CRM-specific language.

## Final run judgment

The run holds. The first-page decision is the structural contribution: a problem-first landing page that defers the product name and category label until recognition has happened. The claim-check caught real issues — the outcome language in Section 4 was the most important — and the corrected draft is tighter on all seven flags. The page is clean and honest about what it can claim. It will not convert a skeptical founder without social proof; that is not a draft problem, it is a proof gap the source material cannot yet close.
