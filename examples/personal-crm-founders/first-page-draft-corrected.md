## Section 1 — The failure mode (hero)

**Heading:** You know who you should follow up with. You just don't know where you left things.

**Body:**

You meant to send an update to that investor two weeks ago. You wanted to check back in with your advisor after that last conversation. You have the name of a customer who said something useful you meant to act on.

None of those happened. You remember the person fine. What faded was the context: what you talked about, what you said you'd do, how long it's been. So you put it off. The relationship drifted a little further.

That is the gap Me CRM is built for.

---

## Section 2 — Why your current system doesn't hold this

**Heading:** Email threads, spreadsheets, and sales CRMs all fail at the same thing.

**Body:**

Email keeps the messages but not the state. To know where you are with someone, you have to search, read back through a thread, piece together what was said and what was implied. That work falls on you every time.

Spreadsheets work until you stop updating them. That tends to happen when things get busy and the deliberate effort the spreadsheet requires runs out of time.

Sales CRMs track deals: stages, pipeline, close probability. That is a different job. A founder managing the relationships that matter most (investors, advisors, early customers, the people who can open doors) is not running a sales process. Forcing those relationships into deal stages adds overhead without solving the actual problem.

---

## Section 3 — What Me CRM tracks

**Heading:** What Me CRM tracks for each relationship

**Body:**

Me CRM is built for a focused set of relationships (the investors, advisors, early customers, and connectors your company depends on most). For each one, it holds a simple state: when you last spoke, what was discussed, what you owe them (open follow-ups), and when to reach back out.

Every Monday, a digest arrives in your inbox showing which relationships have gone quiet: anyone you haven't contacted in 30 days or more. You see the list, you decide what to act on. The digest surfaces the drift so you don't have to track it mentally.

When you do have a conversation, the iOS app lets you capture a note immediately after, before the details fade. The note goes directly to that relationship's card.

Connecting your Gmail account pulls in your existing contact history on setup. The relationship state cards build from there.

Me CRM has no pipeline, no deal stages, and no lead scoring.

## Section 4 — The maintenance question

**Heading:** Will you keep it updated?

**Body:**

This is the real question. You have probably set up a system for this before (a spreadsheet, a notes file, maybe a CRM trial) and stopped using it after a few weeks. The system required you to remember to use it. When things got busy, you stopped.

Me CRM is designed around the assumption that founders do not have spare attention to give a system that requires deliberate, regular maintenance.

The weekly digest arrives whether or not you log in. It shows you what has drifted. You do not have to remember to check. The digest comes to you.

The iOS app is for the two minutes after a meeting. Capture the note before the details fade: a field for the note, and a tap to attach it to the right relationship.

You still have to do the work of the relationships themselves. Me CRM does not do that. What it does is hold the state between conversations so you are not starting from scratch each time you reach out.
